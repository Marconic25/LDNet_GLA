\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We propose an active GLA architecture for a two-dimensional airfoil with a hinged
trailing-edge flap: the aerodynamic loads are supplied by an LDNet, which acts as the internal
model of a preview MPC fed by fused lidar measurements. The LDNet is trained on full-order FSI
co-simulations instead of being derived from a simplified aerodynamic theory, so that it
reproduces the viscous, unsteady and nonlinear response to gust and flap while remaining cheap
enough to be evaluated at every step of the receding horizon and over the whole candidate
grid: the closed loop runs at $\mathcal{O}(10^{1}\text{--}10^{2})$~s of wall-clock per
simulated second on one CPU core, against $\mathcal{O}(10^{3})$~s for the full-order loop on
sixteen, which makes the design loop iterable on the reduced model.

The training set is a campaign of $146$ partitioned co-simulations spanning gust-only,
flap-only and combined excitations. For the depths tested in closed-loop rollout ($L\in\{6,12\}$
at $d_s=10$), the training regime mattered
more than the capacity: teacher forcing alone reached a loss more than twenty times
higher than after fine-tuning, whereas rollout training restored both the stability and the flap
sensitivity that the retained LDNet is verified to have. The damped latent update encodes the
stable aeroelastic equilibrium towards which the airfoil relaxes, a piece of prior knowledge
built into the formulation rather than learned.

The six-signal, ten-latent-state, six-hidden-layer configuration reconstructs the sectional
loads on the held-out test set with an NRMSE as low as $2.4\cdot10^{-3}$ on $F_y$ and
$1.6\cdot10^{-3}$ on $M_z$ open loop, teacher forced; the weights retained for control trade
part of that accuracy for the closed-loop stability and flap-sensitivity that rollout
fine-tuning provides and teacher forcing alone does not. The same reconstruction network also returns the
distributed velocity field away from the airfoil surface, unused by the controller but
available at no additional training cost. In closed loop, with the LDNet as both internal model
and simulated plant, the architecture reduces the peak lift excursion by more than $91\%$ at
the middle of the gradient range ($T_g = \SI{0.7}{\second}$) and by $39$--$95\%$ over the
eighteen cases of a grid inspired by the CS-25.341 discrete gust, without actuator saturation or stability violation;
the effort weight is selected per case over a five-point sweep, so these figures bound the best
tuning of each cell rather than a single fixed tuning over the envelope. The degradations at its
two corners match physical limits rather than a failure of the LDNet: the equivalent-incidence
rate of the sharp--severe gust nears the actuator rate limit, and the pitch constraint binds at
the long-gust corner. Closing the loop on the full-order model corroborates the trend at a lower
absolute level on the two cases checked ($80.7\%$ and $74.6\%$, against $91.9\%$ and $89.1\%$
with the LDNet as the plant), and, against a fitted linear unsteady model on the same full-order
plant, isolates a $52.7$-point advantage ($74.8\%$ against $22.1\%$) attributable to the learned
internal model alone, the strongest evidence that the model, not just the architecture, drives
the alleviation.

Within the sensing model adopted here, the fusion layer keeps the mean reduction on Test~2
within about one percentage point of the noise-free value up to per-shot errors of
$10\%\,W_0$, over six noise realisations per level, and within $3.5$ percentage points under
the range-proportional lidar model. The systematic errors are asymmetric: the controller absorbs an
under-read gust down to $-10\%\,W_0$ with no loss and tolerates a preview time shift of
$\pm20$~ms over the range swept, while an over-read gust degrades the reduction from
$+2\%\,W_0$ and destabilises the loop at $+10\%\,W_0$.

Several aspects of the proposed architecture are not established by this work due to its
proof-of-concept nature: the structural state is assumed directly available, with no observer;
the actuator has deflection and rate limits alone, with no servo dynamics, delay or bandwidth;
$U_\infty$ is held constant, so no generalisation over it is demonstrated; and the control
objective penalises the sectional load available on this two-degree-of-freedom section, whereas
a wing-root bending moment or CS-25.341/CS-25.305 compliance would need the spanwise load
distribution and structural modes of a flexible wing with several control surfaces. Prior
knowledge already enters the LDNet through its formulation --- the damped latent update encodes
the stable equilibrium the airfoil relaxes towards, and the tail-expanding output layer biases
the reconstruction towards the load extremes that size the structure --- and a second-order
latent formulation, giving the dynamics network the mass--spring--damper structure of the
structural equation of motion instead of leaving it to be learned, is a natural extension.
