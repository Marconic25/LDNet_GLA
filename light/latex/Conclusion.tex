\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

This work investigated whether a Latent Dynamics Network can serve as the internal model of an active gust load alleviation system for a two-dimensional aeroelastic airfoil with a trailing-edge flap. A dataset of 146 partitioned FSI co-simulations, spanning gust-only, flap-only and combined excitations, was used to train an LDNet surrogate of the aerodynamic loads. Two findings shape the training methodology: teacher forcing produces models that are accurate in one-step replay but unstable and flap-blind in closed loop, whereas rollout training through the structural dynamics restores both stability and the causal sensitivity of the predicted loads to the flap; a damped latent update encodes the stable aeroelastic equilibrium that the undamped model lacks. The selected surrogate, with ten latent states and the full six-signal input, reconstructs the sectional loads on the held-out test set with $\mathrm{NRMSE}$ of order $10^{-3}$.

Embedded in a preview model predictive controller fed by fused lidar measurements, the surrogate supports effective load alleviation over the CS-25.341 discrete-gust envelope: the peak lift excursion is reduced by more than $81\%$ in the central portion of the gradient range and by $39$--$95\%$ over the whole grid, without actuator saturation and without violating the structural stability check. The degradations at the envelope corners are explained by physical limits, the actuator rate at the sharp--severe corner and the pitch trade-off at the long-gust corner, rather than by the surrogate. The sensing requirements are mild: the fusion layer makes the loop insensitive to white measurement noise up to raw per-shot errors representative of airborne Doppler hardware, and the systematic errors that do break the loop are asymmetric in a favourable direction, so that calibrating the measurement chain to under-read and absorbing timing uncertainty on the delayed side suffice.

The evaluation of the closed loop is performed on the reduced aeroelastic system driven by the true gust, so the natural next step is its verification against the full-order FSI solver in the loop. Further extensions concern the reconstruction of distributed quantities beyond the sectional loads, for which the meshless LDNet formulation is already suited, and the transfer of the methodology from the two-dimensional section to a flexible three-dimensional wing.
