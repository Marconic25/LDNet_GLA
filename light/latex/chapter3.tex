\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

This section reports the results of the study. \Cref{subsec:training_results} assesses the accuracy of the trained LDNet on the held-out test set and documents the hyperparameter selection. \Cref{subsec:mpc_results} evaluates the closed-loop controller over the CS-25 discrete-gust envelope in nominal conditions, and \cref{subsec:noise_results,subsec:systematic_results} probe its robustness to stochastic measurement noise and to systematic sensing and model errors.

\subsection{Surrogate accuracy and hyperparameter tuning}
\label{subsec:training_results}

The analysis of this subsection is deliberately performed in open loop: the networks are driven by the reference structural signals of each trajectory, in the teacher-forcing regime of \cref{subsec:training}, and the reconstruction error of the loads on the held-out test set is measured by the metrics~\eqref{eq:acc_metrics}. Decoupled from the error propagation of an autonomous rollout, this error isolates how accurately the model has learned the load mapping, and it is on this learning accuracy that the selection of the inputs and of $d_s$ is based. All models below are trained with Tikhonov regularization of the network weights~\cite{Regazzoni}: a penalty term, the mean of the squared kernels of $\mathcal{NN}_\mathrm{dyn}$ and $\mathcal{NN}_\mathrm{rec}$ weighted by $\alpha_\mathrm{reg}=3\cdot10^{-4}$, is added to the training loss. Training stops once the validation loss, computed without that term, has not improved for $500$ iterations, and the weights retained are those that attained its minimum. The closed-loop rollout is instead the mode in which the model is used: trained on the rollout loss~\eqref{eq:rollout_loss} with the damped latent update~\eqref{eq:latent_dyn}, the model runs in rollout inside the control loop of the gust-load-alleviation study of \cref{subsec:mpc_results}.

\subsubsection{Input selection}

The first study fixes the outputs $(F_y,M_z)$ and varies the signals fed to the networks, to
establish how much of the structural state the load reconstruction requires. Three
configurations are compared: gust and flap only $\{W,\delta\}$; the same augmented with the
plunge and pitch $\{W,\delta,h,\alpha\}$; and the full structural state
$\{W,\delta,h,\dot h,\alpha,\dot\alpha\}$, which adds the plunge and pitch rates.
Figure~\ref{fig:input_study} reports NRMSE and $1-\rho$ against the latent dimension $d_s$ for
the three sets. The gust-and-flap pair alone is inadequate: without the structural state the
latent dynamics must reproduce the aeroelastic response unaided, and the combined NRMSE is the
largest of the three sets.
Adding the plunge and pitch positions stabilises the reconstruction, but the four-signal set
does not improve beyond $4.0\cdot10^{-3}$ at any latent dimension. The velocity rates
$\dot h,\dot\alpha$ are what admit the accurate regime: only the full six-signal set reaches a
combined NRMSE of $1.3$--$1.4\cdot10^{-3}$. The full
six-signal set is adopted for the remainder of this work.

\subsubsection{Latent-state dimension}
\label{subsubsec:latent_dim}

With the six-signal input fixed, the latent dimension is swept over $d_s\in\{1,3,5,10\}$; for
each value two independent training runs are performed and the run with the
lowest validation loss is retained. \Cref{fig:input_study} and \cref{tab:latent_sweep}
report the resulting test-set accuracy. A single latent state caps the combined NRMSE at
$4.6\cdot10^{-3}$; with more states the optimiser can reach a deep minimum of the validation
loss ($\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{val}\approx 2\cdot10^{-5}$) where the combined NRMSE drops to $1.4\cdot10^{-3}$ at $d_s=3$
and $1.3\cdot10^{-3}$ at $d_s=10$, with $1-\rho\le2\cdot10^{-5}$. The deep minimum is
not reached in every run --- at $d_s=5$ neither of its two runs located it --- a residue of the
sensitivity of the full-batch optimisation to its trajectory, which the multiple-run protocol
mitigates. The latent dimension is set to $d_s=10$, the most accurate configuration, which is
also the value adopted for the closed-loop rollout (\cref{subsec:mpc_results}).

Latent dimensions above $d_s=10$, up to $d_s=20$, were also probed without further gain, and the
reason is architectural rather than statistical: with two hidden layers of seven neurons,
$\mathcal{NN}_\mathrm{dyn}$ bounds the Jacobian of the latent recurrence~\eqref{eq:latent_dyn} to
rank seven, so for $d_s>7$ at least $d_s-7$ latent directions cannot be driven by the network at
all. Left undriven, their eigenvalue in the recurrence sits at $1-\lambda$ --- unity with the
decay switched off --- rather than at a value training can shape, so widening $d_s$ past that
point adds inert state, not usable dynamics. Reaching further requires widening
$\mathcal{NN}_\mathrm{dyn}$ itself, examined next in \cref{subsubsec:depth}.

\subsubsection{Network depth}
\label{subsubsec:depth}

The last architectural degree of freedom is the depth of the two networks. The layer widths are
held fixed at $7$ neurons for $\mathcal{NN}_\mathrm{dyn}$ and $24$ for $\mathcal{NN}_\mathrm{rec}$,
and the total number of hidden layers $L$ is swept starting from $L=6$, the depth used elsewhere
in this work, doubled twice over $L\in\{6,12,24\}$ in the fixed $1{:}2$ split between the two
networks; \cref{tab:depth_arch} lists the three configurations. Every depth is trained at the two
latent dimensions that bracket the sweep of \cref{tab:latent_sweep}, $d_s\in\{1,10\}$, each
configuration twice from independent weight initialisations so that the effect of depth can be
told apart from the run-to-run scatter of the latent sweep, with the six-signal input, the
regularized trainer and the metrics of \cref{eq:acc_metrics} used throughout; \cref{tab:depth_loads}
collects the test-set error of the twelve runs.

Doubling the depth from $L=6$ improves the open-loop reconstruction at both latent dimensions and,
more markedly, its repeatability: $L=12$ reaches the most accurate configuration of the
entire hyperparameter study. Depth does not, however, replace latent capacity — at $d_s=1$ the
deeper network still trails the shallow one at $d_s=10$ — so the latent dimension sets the
accuracy level and the depth decides how reliably the training attains it. The ladder ends at
$L=24$: a plain $\tanh$ stack of this depth, without residual connections or a depth-aware
initialisation, saturates and attenuates the gradient layer after layer~\cite{glorot2010}, and
neither seed reaches a usable minimum, undoing the gains seen at $L=12$.

\subsubsection{Field reconstruction}
\label{subsubsec:field_recon}

Evaluated at query coordinates away from the airfoil surface, $\mathcal{NN}_\mathrm{rec}$ of
\labelcref{eq:reduced_system} returns the distributed field
$\widetilde{\mathbf{y}}=(v_x,v_y,p)$ instead of the sectional loads. This tests the same
latent state far more severely: $F_y$ and $M_z$ are integrals, and tolerate local errors that
cancel, whereas the field has to be right pointwise. Errors are reported per field,
normalised by that field's range, because the combined metric of~\eqref{eq:acc_metrics} mixes
velocities with pressure and is dominated by the pressure scale, and per spatial
region --- near-airfoil, near-wake, far-field and airfoil surface --- because the regions
differ by an order of magnitude in difficulty.

The dominant error of a $\tanh$ coordinate decoder taking $(x,y)$ directly is static.
Splitting it into a time-mean and a fluctuating part attributes $97\%$ of the near-airfoil
$v_x$ error to the time-mean bias (\cref{fig:meansplit_decomp}, red): the decoder smears the
sharp mean boundary-layer gradient and never recovers it, while tracking the gust and flap
fluctuations comparatively well. Since the offending component is static, it need not be
learned at all. The training-set mean field is stored once and the decoder is trained on the
fluctuation, with the min--max normalisation recomputed on the fluctuation ranges and the
mean added back at inference, so the reported errors remain in total-field units. The change
mirrors the mean/POD-mode split standard in projection-based reduced-order
models~\cite{BennerGugercinWillcox2015} and costs nothing: no new inputs, no architecture
change, no extra loss term, and the latent dynamics untouched. The near-airfoil $v_x$ error
falls by a factor $5.7$ on the gust\,+\,flap trajectory and $24$ on the gust-only one; the
static component drops by more than an order of magnitude while the dynamic one is left
essentially unchanged (\cref{fig:meansplit_decomp}, blue). \Cref{tab:meansplit} gives the
region-resolved figures and \cref{fig:meansplit_fields} the effect in physical space: the
error halo that surrounds the whole profile disappears, leaving a localised residual at the
flap gap.

Two further choices follow. Replacing the coordinate decoder with a shift-modulated
sinusoidal representation network in the style of CORAL~\cite{Serrano2023} --- a SIREN base
over the query coordinate, whose per-layer pre-activations are shifted by a small network of
$\mathbf{s}(t)$ and $\boldsymbol{\eta}(t)$, with the latent dynamics unchanged --- lowers the
combined error from $7.2\cdot10^{-3}$ to $6.2\cdot10^{-3}$ over three seeds. The gain exists
only at a low modulation frequency: $\omega_0=10$ improves on the unmodulated decoder,
whereas $\omega_0=30$ and $\omega_0=60$ give $9.8\cdot10^{-3}$ and $1.1\cdot10^{-2}$, worse
than not modulating at all, which places the surviving fluctuation at the smooth end of the
spatial spectrum. The latent dimension is set to $d_s=1$ for the field, against the $d_s=10$
retained for the loads: accuracy is flat across $d_s\in\{1,3,5\}$ within the seed scatter,
but repeatability is not, the seed-to-seed spread of the combined error growing from $1.4\%$
at $d_s=1$ to $11$--$14\%$ above it.

What survives is dynamic, sits on the surface where the flap deflects, and is a physical
event rather than a smoothing artefact. At the gust peak of the gust\,+\,flap trajectory
$23.4\%$ of the near-flap nodes show local flow reversal with respect to their own attached
state, against $2.1\%$ reproduced by the surrogate: there the model misses the sign of the
velocity, not merely its magnitude, and the residual is the transient separation of the flap
cove. It proved robust to a systematic set of modifications spanning decoder architecture
and conditioning, locally gated and mesh-graph decoders, point sampling, optimiser budget,
multiple shooting, dynamics conditioning and loss weighting, none of which improved the
near-flap dynamic error beyond seed scatter.

The residual is nonetheless not a property of the architecture. Every modification above
changed how the surrogate learns; none changed how often it sees the event. Counting, per
training trajectory, the peak fraction of near-flap nodes in reversed flow shows why this
matters: the $15$-trajectory set used above reaches the test case's separation level in
only two of its members, and its median trajectory peaks at $1\%$ reversal, so the regime
is present but represented a handful of times. Retraining the same architecture, unchanged,
on $60$ trajectories --- of which $18$ reach that level --- moves every quantity at once.
Averaged over two seeds the near-flap sign error falls from $21.7\%$ to $14.9\%$ and the
reversed-flow fraction the surrogate reproduces rises from $2.1\%$ to $9.4\%$, against the
$23.4\%$ of the reference, while the near and surface dynamic errors fall by $16\%$ and the
combined error by $19\%$. This is the opposite signature to the modifications listed above,
several of which lowered the combined error while leaving the target region worse. The
limitation is therefore one of how densely the separated regime is sampled, not of the
model class: a global coordinate decoder driven by a one-dimensional latent state
reconstructs the attached field to under $2\%$ of the range of each field, and represents
the separated one to the extent that the training set contains it.

\subsubsection{Rollout model for control}

The retained surrogate combines the results of the studies above: the six-signal input of
\cref{fig:input_study} and $d_s=10$, the most accurate latent dimension of
\cref{subsubsec:latent_dim}. Depth is the one hyperparameter they do not settle by themselves:
\cref{tab:depth_loads} found $L=12$ the most accurate configuration of the entire study, but in
the open-loop, teacher-forced measure of \cref{subsubsec:depth}. Accuracy in that regime is
not what the controller depends on, since the deployed model is not run teacher-forced but in
autonomous rollout, fed only its own predictions once turned loose inside the control loop. $L=6$
and $L=12$ were therefore compared directly on the rollout loss $\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{rollout}$ of
\eqref{eq:rollout_loss} that the deployed model is actually trained and judged on. It sums the
squared structural-state error to the load term over $N_\mathrm{rollout}=800$ autonomous steps,
where each step's error shifts the next one's input away from the training distribution --- the
recurrence that motivates fine-tuning in the first place.

Two values of $\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{rollout}$ mark this comparison for each depth. The
warm-start value takes the weights exactly as left by teacher-forced training, before any
rollout-specific adjustment, and evaluates them in autonomous rollout: it measures how far
teacher-forcing alone carries the model toward closed-loop behaviour. The
fine-tuned value is obtained after continuing training from those same weights directly on
$\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{rollout}$, the second stage of the recipe detailed below. At the warm start
$L=12$ is already worse than $L=6$ ($\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{rollout}=0.0864$ against $0.0373$), and
fine-tuning widens rather than closes the gap: $L=6$ converges cleanly to $0.0114$, while $L=12$
stalls at $0.0400$, $3.5$ times higher, only after its BFGS optimiser aborts a line search
and is forced to restart. Unlike in the open-loop regime, where added depth is a free improvement
in both accuracy and repeatability, in the closed-loop regime it works against the training
objective: a more expressive one-step map is harder to keep well-behaved once composed with itself
for hundreds of steps. The retained surrogate therefore uses $L=6$: verified to be the more stable configuration under autonomous rollout, and the one that runs inside the
control loop of the gust-load-alleviation studies of \cref{subsec:mpc_results}.

At $L=6$, the dynamics network $\mathcal{NN}_\mathrm{dyn}$ has two hidden layers of $7$ neurons and
the reconstruction network $\mathcal{NN}_\mathrm{rec}$ four hidden layers of $24$ neurons, all with
$\tanh$ activation and the tail-compressing nonlinear output layer ($\beta=0.05$); the integration
and reference time steps are $\Delta t=\Delta t_\mathrm{ref}=2\cdot10^{-3}$~s. Training uses the
two-stage teacher-forced schedule of \cref{subsubsec:training_algorithm} ($200$ Adam epochs at
learning rate $10^{-2}$ followed by L-BFGS) with the weight regularization and validation early
stopping described above; the selected model attains the combined $\mathrm{NRMSE}=1.3\cdot10^{-3}$
of \cref{tab:latent_sweep}. Figure~\ref{fig:loss} shows the corresponding training history. The
curve flattens over the last decade of epochs, reaching a training loss
$\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{train}\approx4\cdot10^{-5}$ and a validation loss
$\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{val}\approx2\cdot10^{-5}$ at the retained minimum near epoch $8.8\cdot10^{3}$:
the model is converged rather than truncated by the iteration budget. Training and validation
track each other throughout, with the validation curve sitting slightly below because the
Tikhonov term enters the training objective only; the absence of any late divergence between the
two confirms that the regularized high-capacity model does not overfit. Unlike this teacher-forced
pretraining, whose $\sim10^4$ epochs in \cref{fig:loss} run until the validation-patience rule
above fires, the fine-tuning stage that follows keeps the same architecture and inputs, with the
damped latent update~\eqref{eq:latent_dyn} and $\lambda$ re-optimised for the fine-tuned weights,
but skips the Adam warm-up and runs a much shorter schedule: up to $500$ L-BFGS iterations directly
on $\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{rollout}$, saving the weights whenever the rollout loss improves.

\subsection{Gust load alleviation over the discrete-gust envelope}
\label{subsec:mpc_results}

The controller is evaluated over a parametric grid of 18 gust cases that spans the
CS-25.341 discrete gust envelope: gust amplitude $W_0 \in \{10,\,20,\,30\}$~m/s
and gust duration $T_g \in \{0.3,\,0.4,\,0.5,\,0.7,\,1.0,\,1.2\}$~s.
Each case is located in the aerodynamically relevant parameter space by two quantities derived
from the gust parameters of \cref{eq:gust}: the gust gradient distance $H = U_\infty T_g/2$, the
length with which CS-25.341 parametrises the discrete-gust envelope and is therefore reported in
feet, and the reduced frequency $k = \pi\,c_{\mathrm{ref}}/(U_\infty T_g)$.

The primary performance metric is the fractional reduction of the peak
lift-coefficient excursion with respect to the open-loop reference:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{CLred} = \frac{\max_t |C_L^{\mathrm{OL}}(t) - C_{L,\mathrm{trim}}|
                         - \max_t |C_L^{\mathrm{CL}}(t) - C_{L,\mathrm{trim}}|}
                         {\max_t |C_L^{\mathrm{OL}}(t) - C_{L,\mathrm{trim}}|}
                    \times 100\%,
\label{eq:clred}
\end{equation}
where the maximum is taken over the gust window $t \in [0,\, T_g + 0.5\,\mathrm{s}]$ and
$C_{L,\mathrm{trim}}$ is the target the controller drives the lift towards, the same
reference whose deviations the MPC cost~\eqref{eq:mpc_cost} penalises: CLred is therefore
the fraction of the open-loop excursion about that target that the controller removes.
A secondary stability check excludes any tuning that causes any of
$\dot\alpha$, $\ddot\alpha$, or $\ddot h$ to exceed three times the
corresponding open-loop peak. For each gust case the effort weight
of the cost~\eqref{eq:mpc_cost} is swept over
$R \in \{10^{-4},\,3\cdot10^{-4},\,10^{-3},\,3\cdot10^{-3},\,10^{-2}\}$,
five half-decade steps, and the selected value, written $R^{\star}$, is the
one maximising CLred among those that satisfy the check. The weight is
therefore tuned case by case rather than held fixed over the envelope, and
$R^{\star}$ is reported for every case alongside CLred.
The pitch ratio, defined as the ratio of the closed-loop to open-loop peak
pitch excursion, is monitored alongside CLred to make explicit any structural
load trade-off introduced by the flap. In this nominal evaluation the sensing
chain is noise-free, so the preview vector coincides with the sampled true

\subsubsection{Performance over the discrete-gust envelope}

\Cref{fig:mpc_traces} shows the nominal closed-loop response at two
representative gust cases, the severe short gust ($W_0 = 30$~m/s,
$T_g = 0.4$~s) and a longer one ($W_0 = 30$~m/s, $T_g = 0.7$~s). The preview
lets the flap deploy while the gust is still building up, and the closed-loop
lift remains confined to a narrow band around trim: the peak excursion is
reduced by $80.5\%$ and $91.8\%$ respectively, with at most $10.5^{\circ}$ of

\subsection{Robustness to lidar measurement noise}
\label{subsec:noise_results}

The stochastic robustness of the pipeline is assessed with both noise
models of \cref{subsec:mpc_arch}: the constant-level model drives the
amplitude sweep of this subsection, at the severe case ($W_0 = 30$~m/s,
$T_g = 0.4$~s), while the range-proportional lidar model enters the
multi-case check below. The sweep applies
the measurement model~\eqref{eq:meas_model} with the constant per-sample
level $\sigma_j = \sigma$: at every control instant $t_n$ each of the
$J_{\max}$ raw samples is corrupted by its own error draw,
\begin{equation*}
y_j(t_n) = W\bigl(t_n + j\,\Delta t\bigr) + \varepsilon_{nj}, \qquad
\varepsilon_{nj} \sim \mathcal{N}\bigl(0,\,\sigma^2\bigr), \qquad
\frac{\sigma}{W_0} \in \{0, 1, 2, 5, 10, 20\}\%,
\end{equation*}
with the errors drawn independently across nodes and control instants:
white noise of up to $6$~m/s per shot. The noisy samples enter the
fusion sums of \cref{eq:invvar} exactly as drawn, and the aeroelastic
system keeps advancing with the true gust $W(t)$: the noise reaches the
control loop only through the preview vector $\widehat{W}_k$ in the
horizon cost of \cref{eq:mpc_cost}. Since the outcome depends on the particular random
sequence drawn, each level is simulated six times with independent noise
realisations, and the statistics are taken over the six repetitions.

\Cref{fig:noise_white} reports the resulting reduction together with the
error actually delivered to the controller after fusion,
$\sigma_{\mathrm{del}} = \mathrm{std}\bigl(\widehat{W}_1(t) - W(t+\Delta t)\bigr)$,
the standard deviation of the residual on the first preview node over the
control steps of the evaluation window, averaged over the realisations.
Since each gust
value is measured $J_{\max} = 50$ times with independent errors before
reaching the wing, the inverse-variance average of \cref{eq:invvar}
shrinks the per-sample error by roughly $\sqrt{J_{\max}} \approx 7$: at
$\sigma = 0.6$~m/s the delivered error is $0.07$~m/s, about one order of
magnitude below the raw level. The noise that actually reaches the horizon
cost~\eqref{eq:mpc_cost} is therefore far smaller than the nominal
$\sigma$, and as a result the mean reduction remains within about one
percentage point of the noise-free value up to $\sigma = 10\%\,W_0 =
3$~m/s---the upper end of the per-shot error of airborne Doppler hardware
discussed in \cref{subsec:mpc_arch}. Only at $\sigma = 20\%\,W_0$ does the
spread across realisations open appreciably (worst realisation $+53.0\%$,
two flagged out of six).

    \caption{Peak-load reduction under white measurement noise on the raw

A multi-case check verifies that this tolerance is not a property of a
single operating point: with one fixed, untuned configuration
($R = 3\times 10^{-4}$), three gust cases spanning the envelope are run
under flat noise at $\sigma = 2\%\,W_0$ and under the range-proportional
lidar model of \cref{subsec:mpc_arch}, whose raw line-of-sight error
(1--3~m/s) reaches $10$--$30\%$ of $W_0$ at the gentlest case. In every
case the mean reduction stays within $1.5$ percentage points of the
corresponding noise-free value, with no stability flag in any realisation:
noise-free/flat/lidar CLred is $79.6\%/79.6\%/81.1\%$ at the severe case
($W_0=30$~m/s, $T_g=0.4$~s), $93.5\%/93.5\%/92.0\%$ at $W_0=10$~m/s,
$T_g=0.7$~s, and $91.5\%/91.5\%/91.5\%$ at $W_0=30$~m/s, $T_g=0.7$~s.

\subsection{Systematic errors and model mismatch}
\label{subsec:systematic_results}

Zero-mean noise is, however, the error the fusion layer is built to remove;
systematic errors pass through it unaffected. On the sensing side they are
modelled by corrupting the raw samples of \cref{eq:meas_model} as
\begin{equation*}
y_j(t_n) = W\bigl(t_n + j\,\Delta t + \tau\bigr) + b + \varepsilon_{nj},
\end{equation*}
with one error active at a time: a calibration bias $b = \beta\,W_0$,
identical for every sample, and a time shift $\tau$ of the measured
profile, positive when the gust appears in the preview earlier
than it arrives. Since the fusion
\eqref{eq:invvar}--\eqref{eq:tikhonov} is linear in the samples, averaging
leaves both terms untouched: the delivered preview is
$\widehat{W}_k \approx W(t + k\Delta t + \tau) + b$, an error that
enters every term of the horizon cost~\eqref{eq:mpc_cost} coherently
instead of averaging down.

\Cref{fig:noise_bias_timing} sweeps the two errors, again at the severe
case. In
both panels the curve is a deterministic sweep with the systematic error
alone ($\varepsilon_{nj} = 0$); at $b = \pm 5\%\,W_0$ and
$\tau = \pm 10$~ms the flat noise of the previous subsection
($\sigma = 2\%\,W_0$, six realisations) is superimposed on the systematic
error to probe the combination of the two error classes. On the tolerated
side the noisy means reproduce the deterministic values within a fraction
of a percentage point with no flag, so the two classes do not interact;
past the failure threshold every realisation raises the flag. Both axes
are strongly asymmetric. A calibration bias that
over-reads the gust collapses the controller already at
$+2\%\,W_0 = +0.6$~m/s ($+23.5\%$, flagged), while under-reading is nearly
free down to $-5\%\,W_0$ ($+73.9\%$, no flag): an over-read gust keeps the
flap deployed against a disturbance that never materialises, and the
self-induced transient violates the pitch-rate bound. For the systematic
time shift of the preview, a delayed preview costs
$2$ percentage points at $-10$~ms ($+78.6\%$, no flag), because the receding
horizon re-plans from the measured state at every step, whereas an
anticipated preview is flagged already at $+4$~ms and collapses at $+10$~ms
($+18.0\%$)---the flap acts against a gust that has not yet arrived.

    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{Thesis_article_format/Chapters/Chapter 3/Images/fig_ch3_bias_timing.png}
    \caption{Robustness to the two critical systematic errors at the severe

These asymmetries translate into a practical specification for keeping
systematic errors harmless. First, the measurement chain should be
calibrated to under-read: residual calibration uncertainty must sit on
the side where the gust is underestimated, which the controller absorbs
down to $-5\%\,W_0$, not on the over-reading side that collapses it at
$+2\%\,W_0$. Second, residual timing uncertainty must be absorbed on the
delayed side of the acquisition chain: the receding horizon re-plans
from the measured state and tolerates a late preview, whereas an
anticipated one commands the flap against a gust that has not yet
arrived.
