\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

This section reports the results of the study. \Cref{subsec:training_results} assesses the accuracy of the trained LDNet on the held-out test set and documents the hyperparameter selection. \Cref{subsec:mpc_results} evaluates the closed-loop controller over the CS-25 discrete-gust envelope in nominal conditions, and \cref{subsec:noise_results,subsec:systematic_results} probe its robustness to stochastic measurement noise and to systematic sensing and model errors.

\subsection{Surrogate accuracy and hyperparameter tuning}
\label{subsec:training_results}

The analysis of this subsection is deliberately performed in open loop: the networks are driven by the reference structural signals of each trajectory, in the teacher-forcing regime of \cref{subsec:training}, and the reconstruction error of the loads on the held-out test set is measured by the metrics~\eqref{eq:acc_metrics}. Decoupled from the error propagation of an autonomous rollout, this error isolates how accurately the model has learned the load mapping, and it is on this learning accuracy that the selection of the inputs and of $d_s$ is based. All models below are trained with Tikhonov regularization of the network weights~\cite{Regazzoni}: a penalty term, the mean of the squared kernels of $\mathcal{NN}_\mathrm{dyn}$ and $\mathcal{NN}_\mathrm{rec}$ weighted by $\alpha_\mathrm{reg}=3\cdot10^{-4}$, is added to the training loss. Training is stopped at the minimum of the validation loss, which is computed without that term. The closed-loop rollout is instead the mode in which the model is used: trained on the rollout loss~\eqref{eq:rollout_loss} with the damped latent update~\eqref{eq:latent_dyn}, the model runs in rollout inside the control loop of the gust-load-alleviation study of \cref{subsec:mpc_results}.

\subsubsection{Input selection}

The first study fixes the outputs $(F_y,M_z)$ and varies the signals fed to the networks, to
establish how much of the structural state the load reconstruction requires. Three
configurations are compared: gust and flap only $\{W,\delta\}$; the same augmented with the
plunge and pitch $\{W,\delta,h,\alpha\}$; and the full structural state
$\{W,\delta,h,\dot h,\alpha,\dot\alpha\}$, which adds the plunge and pitch rates.
Figure~\ref{fig:input_study} reports NRMSE and $1-\rho$ against the latent dimension $d_s$ for
the three sets. The gust-and-flap pair alone is inadequate: without the structural state the
latent dynamics must reproduce the aeroelastic response unaided, and the combined NRMSE is the
largest and most erratic of the three sets (between $3.9\cdot10^{-3}$ and $3.2\cdot10^{-2}$).
Adding the plunge and pitch positions stabilises the reconstruction, but the four-signal set
does not improve beyond $4.0\cdot10^{-3}$ at any latent dimension. The velocity rates
$\dot h,\dot\alpha$ are what admit the accurate regime: only the full six-signal set reaches a
combined NRMSE of $1.3$--$1.4\cdot10^{-3}$, three times below the four-signal floor. The full
six-signal set is adopted for the remainder of this work.

\subsubsection{Latent-state dimension}

With the six-signal input fixed, the latent dimension is swept over $d_s\in\{1,3,5,10\}$; for
each value two independent training runs are performed (three at $d_s=10$) and the run with the
lowest validation loss is retained. \Cref{fig:input_study} and \cref{tab:latent_sweep}
report the resulting test-set accuracy. A single latent state caps the combined NRMSE at
$4.6\cdot10^{-3}$; with more states the optimiser can reach a deep minimum of the validation
loss ($\approx 2\cdot10^{-5}$) where the combined NRMSE drops to $1.4\cdot10^{-3}$ at $d_s=3$
and $1.3\cdot10^{-3}$ at $d_s=10$, with $1-\rho\le2\cdot10^{-5}$. The weight regularization is
what makes this capacity usable. A larger $d_s$ multiplies the degrees of freedom of the
latent dynamics, and on a training set of $100$ trajectories the unregularized optimiser
spends them on trajectory-specific detail that does not transfer to unseen data; the Tikhonov
penalty shrinks the weights and thereby bounds the local gains of the learned maps, keeping
the latent dynamics smooth, so the additional states are spent on the aeroelastic response
shared across trajectories. Without it the higher-dimensional models overfit the latent
dynamics and lose to $d_s=1$, consistently with the role of tuned L2 regularization in
restoring a monotone benefit of model capacity~\cite{nakkiran2021optimal}. The deep minimum is
not reached in every run --- at $d_s=5$ neither of its two runs located it --- a residue of the
sensitivity of the full-batch optimisation to its trajectory, which the multiple-run protocol
mitigates. The latent dimension is set to $d_s=10$, the most accurate configuration, which is
also the value adopted for the closed-loop rollout (\cref{subsec:mpc_results}).

A configuration at $d_s=20$, trained with the same protocol and three restarts, shows the same
behaviour at the upper end of the range. Its best validation loss is $2.63\cdot10^{-4}$,
against $2.09\cdot10^{-5}$ at $d_s=3$ and $1.94\cdot10^{-5}$ at $d_s=10$: the deep minimum was
reached in none of the three restarts, and the value sits with those of the $d_s=1$ and
$d_s=5$ cells, which missed it as well. The combined test NRMSE is $7.5\cdot10^{-3}$ with six
inputs, $5.1\cdot10^{-3}$ with four and $1.1\cdot10^{-2}$ with two. Two of the three six-input
restarts stop before $300$ iterations on a memoryless solution, with a combined NRMSE of
$5.9\cdot10^{-2}$ and $\rho(F_y)<0.3$.

\subsubsection{Network depth}

The last architectural degree of freedom is the depth of the two networks. The layer widths
are held at their baseline values, $7$ neurons for $\mathcal{NN}_\mathrm{dyn}$ and $24$ for
$\mathcal{NN}_\mathrm{rec}$, and the total number of hidden layers $L$ is doubled twice over
$L\in\{6,12,24\}$, split between the two networks in the fixed ratio $1{:}2$ of the baseline
architecture; \cref{tab:depth_arch} lists the three configurations and the number of trainable
weights $P$ that each of them carries. Every depth is trained at the two latent dimensions that
bracket the sweep of \cref{tab:latent_sweep}, $d_s\in\{1,10\}$, and each of the six resulting
configurations is trained twice: the two runs share data, architecture and training schedule,
and differ only in the random initialisation of the network weights, so that the effect of
depth can be told apart from the run-to-run scatter seen in the latent sweep. The six-signal
input, the regularized trainer and the metrics of \cref{eq:acc_metrics} are those used above.
\Cref{tab:depth_loads} collects the test-set error of the twelve runs.

Doubling the baseline depth improves the reconstruction at both latent dimensions, by about one
fifth at $d_s=1$ and by about one third at $d_s=10$, where $L=12$ is the most accurate
configuration of the entire hyperparameter study with a combined $\mathrm{NRMSE}=9\cdot10^{-4}$.
The stronger effect is on the spread between the two runs: at $L=6$ the two $d_s=10$ runs land
at $1.35\cdot10^{-3}$ and $6.17\cdot10^{-3}$, a factor of $4.6$ apart, whereas at $L=12$ they
agree to within $6\%$, so the added layers remove the initialisation sensitivity that the
multiple-run protocol of the latent sweep was introduced to mitigate. Depth does not, however,
replace latent capacity: at $d_s=1$ the deeper network stops at $2.9\cdot10^{-3}$, twice the
error that $d_s=10$ already reaches at the baseline depth, so the latent dimension sets the
accuracy level and the depth decides how reliably the training attains it.

The ladder ends at $L=24$, where the combined NRMSE rises to between $5.2\cdot10^{-3}$ and
$2.2\cdot10^{-2}$, above every baseline value at the same latent dimension and still strongly
dependent on the initialisation: a plain $\tanh$ stack of this depth, without residual
connections or a depth-aware initialisation, saturates and attenuates the gradient layer after
layer~\cite{glorot2010}, and neither run reaches a usable minimum within the iteration budget. The closed-loop model of \cref{subsec:mpc_results} keeps the baseline $L=6$;
what the sweep adds is that this depth is a conservative choice rather than an optimal one, and
that a moderate deepening to $L=12$, at roughly twice the number of weights, would improve the
open-loop load reconstruction by about a third.

\subsubsection{Rollout model for control}

The retained surrogate uses $d_s=10$ latent states, the six input signals and the freestream
parameter $U_\infty$. The dynamics network $\mathcal{NN}_\mathrm{dyn}$ has two hidden layers of
$7$ neurons and the reconstruction network $\mathcal{NN}_\mathrm{rec}$ four hidden layers of
$24$ neurons, all with $\tanh$ activation and the tail-compressing nonlinear output layer
($\beta=0.05$); the integration and reference time steps are
$\Delta t=\Delta t_\mathrm{ref}=2\cdot10^{-3}$~s. Training uses the two-stage teacher-forced
schedule of \cref{subsubsec:training_algorithm} ($200$ Adam epochs at learning rate $10^{-2}$
followed by L-BFGS) with the weight regularization and validation early stopping described
above; the selected model attains the combined $\mathrm{NRMSE}=1.3\cdot10^{-3}$ of
\cref{tab:latent_sweep}. Figure~\ref{fig:loss} shows the corresponding training history. The
two-stage schedule is clearly delineated: during the $200$ Adam epochs the objective stalls on
a plateau near $1.5\cdot10^{-2}$, and it is the subsequent L-BFGS phase that resumes the descent
and lowers the loss by roughly three orders of magnitude. The curve flattens over the last
decade of epochs, reaching a training loss $\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{train}\approx4\cdot10^{-5}$ and a
validation loss $\mathcal{L}_\mathrm{val}\approx2\cdot10^{-5}$
at the retained minimum near epoch $8.8\cdot10^{3}$: the model is converged rather
than truncated by the iteration budget. Training and validation track each other throughout,
with the validation curve sitting slightly below because the Tikhonov term enters the training
objective only; the absence of any late divergence between the two confirms that the regularized
high-capacity model does not overfit. Starting from the configuration selected by the teacher-forced study, the model deployed in the
controller is trained anew in closed-loop rollout on the loss~\eqref{eq:rollout_loss} with the
damped latent update~\eqref{eq:latent_dyn}: same architecture, inputs and latent dimension,
with the decay coefficient $\lambda$ optimised for the selected configuration. It is this
rollout model that runs inside the control loop of the gust-load-alleviation studies of
\cref{subsec:mpc_results}.

\subsection{Gust load alleviation over the discrete-gust envelope}
\label{subsec:mpc_results}

The controller is evaluated over a parametric grid of 18 gust cases that spans the
CS-25.341 discrete gust envelope: gust amplitude $W_0 \in \{10,\,20,\,30\}$~m/s
and gust duration $T_g \in \{0.3,\,0.4,\,0.5,\,0.7,\,1.0,\,1.2\}$~s.
Each case is located in the aerodynamically relevant parameter space by two derived
quantities. The gust gradient distance $H = U_\infty T_g / 2$ is the distance flown
while the gust builds up to its peak; it is the length with which CS-25.341
parametrises the discrete-gust envelope, and is reported in feet for that reason. The
reduced frequency $k = \pi\,c_{\mathrm{ref}} / (U_\infty T_g)$ is the gust circular
frequency $2\pi/T_g$ made nondimensional with the chord and the freestream speed, and
measures how unsteady the aerodynamic response to the gust is.

The primary performance metric is the fractional reduction of the peak
lift-coefficient excursion with respect to the open-loop reference:
\begin{equation}
    \mathrm{CLred} = \frac{\max_t |C_L^{\mathrm{OL}}(t) - C_{L,\mathrm{trim}}|
                         - \max_t |C_L^{\mathrm{CL}}(t) - C_{L,\mathrm{trim}}|}
                         {\max_t |C_L^{\mathrm{OL}}(t) - C_{L,\mathrm{trim}}|}
                    \times 100\%,
\label{eq:clred}
\end{equation}
where the maximum is taken over the gust window $t \in [0,\, T_g + 0.5\,\mathrm{s}]$ and
$C_{L,\mathrm{trim}}$ is the target the controller drives the lift towards, the same
reference whose deviations the MPC cost~\eqref{eq:mpc_cost} penalises: CLred is therefore
the fraction of the open-loop excursion about that target that the controller removes.
A secondary stability check excludes any tuning that causes any of
$\dot\alpha$, $\ddot\alpha$, or $\ddot h$ to exceed three times the
corresponding open-loop peak. For each gust case the effort weight
of the cost~\eqref{eq:mpc_cost} is swept over
$R \in \{10^{-4},\,3\cdot10^{-4},\,10^{-3},\,3\cdot10^{-3},\,10^{-2}\}$,
five half-decade steps, and the selected value, written $R^{\star}$, is the
one maximising CLred among those that satisfy the check. The weight is
therefore tuned case by case rather than held fixed over the envelope, and
$R^{\star}$ is reported for every case alongside CLred.
The pitch ratio, defined as the ratio of the closed-loop to open-loop peak
pitch excursion, is monitored alongside CLred to make explicit any structural
load trade-off introduced by the flap. In this nominal evaluation the sensing
chain is noise-free, so the preview vector coincides with the sampled true
gust profile~\eqref{eq:gust}.

aerodynamic and actuator parameters shared by every run of this section;
the symbols are those of the structural model~\eqref{eq:eom}--\eqref{eq:matrices}.
The structural and flap values are adapted from the typical-section aeroelastic
benchmark of Hodges and Pierce~\cite{hodges2011aeroelasticity}.

\subsubsection{Performance over the discrete-gust envelope}

\Cref{fig:mpc_traces} shows the nominal closed-loop response at two
representative gust cases, the severe short gust ($W_0 = 30$~m/s,
$T_g = 0.4$~s) and a longer one ($W_0 = 30$~m/s, $T_g = 0.7$~s). The preview
lets the flap deploy while the gust is still building up, and the closed-loop
lift remains confined to a narrow band around trim: the peak excursion is
reduced by $80.5\%$ and $91.8\%$ respectively, with at most $10.5^{\circ}$ of
deflection and closed-loop pitch excursions comparable to or smaller than the
open-loop ones (ratios $1.02$ and $0.75$).

The full grid is reported in \cref{tab:cs25_grid}. In
the central portion of the gradient range ($T_g = 0.5$--$0.7$~s) the
controller removes more than $81\%$ of the peak excursion at every amplitude,
and more than $88\%$ for $W_0 \le 20$~m/s. Performance degrades toward both
ends of the envelope, for different reasons. At the sharp--severe corner
($W_0 = 30$~m/s, $T_g = 0.3$~s) the reduction is limited to $39.4\%$ and is
only weakly sensitive to the effort weight (below $5$ percentage points of
variation over two decades of $R$, with no stability violation): the peak
gust slope $\pi W_0/T_g$ corresponds to an equivalent-incidence rate
$\dot{W}/U_\infty \approx 225~^{\circ}$/s, comparable to the actuator rate
limit itself, so the flap---aerodynamically less effective than
incidence---cannot track the gust ramp regardless of the tuning. At the
long-gust corner the stability check becomes the active constraint: the
large sustained deflection required to cancel a quasi-steady gust drives the
pitch degree of freedom, whose open-loop peaks---and hence the $3\times$
bounds---are small for slow gusts;
aggressive tunings amplify the pitch excursion (ratios up to $1.9$) and
violate the $\dot\alpha$ bound, so the selected weight rises to
$R^{\star} = 10^{-2}$ and the reduction settles at $41.7\%$
($W_0 = 30$~m/s) and $58.2\%$ ($W_0 = 20$~m/s) at $T_g = 1.2$~s. Across the
whole grid the actuator never saturates ($|\delta|_{\max} \le 12.2^{\circ} <
\delta_{\max}$) and no selected tuning violates the stability check; the
pitch ratio spans $0.57$--$1.47$.

\subsection{Robustness to lidar measurement noise}
\label{subsec:noise_results}

The stochastic robustness of the pipeline is assessed with both noise
models of \cref{subsec:mpc_arch}: the constant-level model drives the
amplitude sweep of this subsection, at the severe case ($W_0 = 30$~m/s,
$T_g = 0.4$~s), while the range-proportional lidar model enters the
multi-case check of \cref{fig:noise_cases} below. The sweep applies
the measurement model~\eqref{eq:meas_model} with the constant per-sample
level $\sigma_j = \sigma$: at every control instant $t_n$ each of the
$J_{\max}$ raw samples is corrupted by its own error draw,
\begin{equation*}
y_j(t_n) = W\bigl(t_n + j\,\Delta t\bigr) + \varepsilon_{nj}, \qquad
\varepsilon_{nj} \sim \mathcal{N}\bigl(0,\,\sigma^2\bigr), \qquad
\frac{\sigma}{W_0} \in \{0, 1, 2, 5, 10, 20\}\%,
\end{equation*}
with the errors drawn independently across nodes and control instants:
white noise of up to $6$~m/s per shot. The noisy samples enter the
fusion sums of \cref{eq:invvar} exactly as drawn, and the aeroelastic
system keeps advancing with the true gust $W(t)$: the noise reaches the
control loop only through the preview vector $\widehat{W}_k$ in the
horizon cost of \cref{eq:mpc_cost}. Since the outcome depends on the particular random
sequence drawn, each level is simulated six times with independent noise
realisations, and the statistics are taken over the six repetitions.

\Cref{fig:noise_white} reports the resulting reduction together with the
error actually delivered to the controller after fusion,
$\sigma_{\mathrm{del}} = \mathrm{std}\bigl(\widehat{W}_1(t) - W(t+\Delta t)\bigr)$,
the standard deviation of the residual on the first preview node over the
control steps of the evaluation window, averaged over the realisations.
Since each gust
value is measured $J_{\max} = 50$ times with independent errors before
reaching the wing, the inverse-variance average of \cref{eq:invvar}
shrinks the per-sample error by roughly $\sqrt{J_{\max}} \approx 7$: at
$\sigma = 0.6$~m/s the delivered error is $0.07$~m/s, about one order of
magnitude below the raw level. The noise that actually reaches the horizon
cost~\eqref{eq:mpc_cost} is therefore far smaller than the nominal
$\sigma$, and as a result the mean reduction remains within about one
percentage point of the noise-free value up to $\sigma = 10\%\,W_0 =
3$~m/s---the upper end of the per-shot error of airborne Doppler hardware
discussed in \cref{subsec:mpc_arch}. Only at $\sigma = 20\%\,W_0$ does the
spread across realisations open appreciably (worst realisation $+53.0\%$,
two flagged out of six).

\Cref{fig:noise_cases} verifies that this tolerance is not a property of a
single operating point: with one fixed, untuned configuration
($R = 3\times 10^{-4}$), three gust cases spanning the envelope are run
under flat noise at $\sigma = 2\%\,W_0$ and under the range-proportional
lidar model of \cref{subsec:mpc_arch}, whose raw line-of-sight error
(1--3~m/s) reaches $10$--$30\%$ of $W_0$ at the gentlest case. In every
case the mean reduction stays within $1.5$ percentage points of the
corresponding noise-free value, with no stability flag in any realisation.

\subsection{Systematic errors and model mismatch}
\label{subsec:systematic_results}

Zero-mean noise is, however, the error the fusion layer is built to remove;
systematic errors pass through it unaffected. On the sensing side they are
modelled by corrupting the raw samples of \cref{eq:meas_model} as
\begin{equation*}
y_j(t_n) = W\bigl(t_n + j\,\Delta t + \tau\bigr) + b + \varepsilon_{nj},
\end{equation*}
with one error active at a time: a calibration bias $b = \beta\,W_0$,
identical for every sample, and a time shift $\tau$ of the measured
profile, positive when the gust appears in the preview earlier
than it arrives. Since the fusion
\eqref{eq:invvar}--\eqref{eq:tikhonov} is linear in the samples, averaging
leaves both terms untouched: the delivered preview is
$\widehat{W}_k \approx W(t + k\Delta t + \tau) + b$, an error that
enters every term of the horizon cost~\eqref{eq:mpc_cost} coherently
instead of averaging down.

\Cref{fig:noise_bias_timing} sweeps the two errors, again at the severe
case. In
both panels the curve is a deterministic sweep with the systematic error
alone ($\varepsilon_{nj} = 0$); at $b = \pm 5\%\,W_0$ and
$\tau = \pm 10$~ms the flat noise of the previous subsection
($\sigma = 2\%\,W_0$, six realisations) is superimposed on the systematic
error to probe the combination of the two error classes. On the tolerated
side the noisy means reproduce the deterministic values within a fraction
of a percentage point with no flag, so the two classes do not interact;
past the failure threshold every realisation raises the flag. Both axes
are strongly asymmetric. A calibration bias that
over-reads the gust collapses the controller already at
$+2\%\,W_0 = +0.6$~m/s ($+23.5\%$, flagged), while under-reading is nearly
free down to $-5\%\,W_0$ ($+73.9\%$, no flag): an over-read gust keeps the
flap deployed against a disturbance that never materialises, and the
self-induced transient violates the pitch-rate bound. For the systematic
time shift of the preview, a delayed preview costs
$2$ percentage points at $-10$~ms ($+78.6\%$, no flag), because the receding
horizon re-plans from the measured state at every step, whereas an
anticipated preview is flagged already at $+4$~ms and collapses at $+10$~ms
($+18.0\%$)---the flap acts against a gust that has not yet arrived.

These asymmetries translate into a practical specification for keeping
systematic errors harmless. First, the measurement chain should be
calibrated to under-read: residual calibration uncertainty must sit on
the side where the gust is underestimated, which the controller absorbs
down to $-5\%\,W_0$, not on the over-reading side that collapses it at
$+2\%\,W_0$. Second, residual timing uncertainty must be absorbed on the
delayed side of the acquisition chain: the receding horizon re-plans
from the measured state and tolerates a late preview, whereas an
anticipated one commands the flap against a gust that has not yet
arrived.
