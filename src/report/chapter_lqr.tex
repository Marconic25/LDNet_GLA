\subsection{Closed-Loop Results with LQR}
%Time histories of plunge, pitch and flap deflection under LQR control, comparison with the open-loop baseline, sensitivity to Q/R weights, discussion of trade-off between oscillation reduction and control effort.

Gust load alleviation (GLA) aims to reduce the structural response of an
aeroelastic system to atmospheric disturbances through active flap deflection.
Model Predictive Control (MPC) provides excellent performance by rolling out
an explicit nonlinear preview over a receding horizon, but requires solving an
online optimisation at every timestep.
Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) offers an attractive alternative: the optimal
feedback gain is computed offline once, at the expense of restricting
optimality to the neighbourhood of the trim point.

The plant considered here is the \emph{LDNet} model---a data-driven aeroelastic
emulator that couples a two-degree-of-freedom (2-DOF) structural wing with a
neural-network aerodynamic surrogate.
Because the aerodynamic model is a trained neural network, closed-form
Jacobians are unavailable; they are instead obtained through \emph{automatic
differentiation} (AD) of the full discrete-time propagator.
The resulting linearised system is then used to solve the Discrete Algebraic
Riccati Equation (DARE) and extract the optimal gain matrix $\mK$.

\subsubsection{LQR Architecture and Role of the Observer}
\label{sec:lqr_arch}
% ================================================================

\paragraph{Block-diagram overview.}
Figure~\ref{fig:lqr_block} shows the closed-loop architecture.
At each timestep $k$ the controller receives the estimated augmented state
$\hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_k = [\hat{\vxi}_k^\top,\hat{W}_k]^\top$ from the
\emph{observer} and computes the flap command via the linear law
\eqref{eq:law}.
The plant (LDNet + structural dynamics) then evolves under that command and
produces the next measurable outputs ($\ddot{h}$, $\ddot{\alpha}$, $C_L$), which feed
back into the observer.

\paragraph{Why the observer is needed.}
The gain law $\delta_k = -\mK\hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_k$ requires all six components
of $\hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_k = [\hat{h},\hat{\dot{h}},\hat{\alpha},\hat{\dot{\alpha}},\hat{z},\hat{W}]^\top$
at each timestep.
Two observer implementations are compared, differing in their sensor
assumptions:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Heuristic observer}: only two inertial signals are available,
        the heave acceleration $\ddot{h}$ and the pitch angular acceleration
        $\ddot{\alpha}$ (both from onboard accelerometers), plus the lift
        coefficient $C_L$ (pressure tap or force balance).
        No angle or rate sensor is assumed.
  \item \textbf{EKF observer}: additionally assumes an angle-of-attack (AoA)
        sensor providing $\alpha$ and $\dot{\alpha}$ directly, giving the
        measurement vector $\mathbf{y} = [\ddot{h},\,\alpha,\,\dot{\alpha}]^\top$.
\end{itemize}
In both cases $\hat{z}$ and $\hat{W}$ are not measured and must be
reconstructed.
The remaining states are recovered as described below.

\paragraph{Structural state reconstruction.}
All four structural states $h$, $\dot{h}$, $\alpha$, $\dot{\alpha}$ are
unobserved and reconstructed by applying a \emph{leaky kinematic integrator}
to each measured acceleration:
\begin{align}
  \hat{\dot{h}}_{k+1}       &= \ell\,\hat{\dot{h}}_k       + \ddot{h}_k\,\Delta t,       \label{eq:leak_hd}\\
  \hat{h}_{k+1}             &= \ell\,\hat{h}_k             + \hat{\dot{h}}_{k+1}\,\Delta t, \label{eq:leak_h}\\
  \hat{\dot{\alpha}}_{k+1}  &= \ell\,\hat{\dot{\alpha}}_k  + \ddot{\alpha}_k\,\Delta t,  \label{eq:leak_ad}\\
  \hat{\alpha}_{k+1}        &= \ell\,\hat{\alpha}_k        + \hat{\dot{\alpha}}_{k+1}\,\Delta t, \label{eq:leak_a}
\end{align}
where $\ell = 1 - \Delta t/\tau$, $\tau = 5\,\text{s}$, is the leak factor.
The multiplicative leak prevents unbounded growth due to accelerometer bias or
numerical drift, at the cost of a slow low-frequency attenuation (corner
frequency $f_c = 1/(2\pi\tau) \approx 0.03\,\text{Hz}$, well below the
aeroelastic modes at $\sim\!3\,\text{Hz}$).

\paragraph{Gust estimation.}
The gust velocity $\hat{W}_k$ is not measured directly but inferred by
\emph{inverting the aerodynamic model}: given the current state estimate
$\hat{\vxi}_k$, flap angle $\delta_k$, and measured lift $C_{L,k}^{\rm meas}$,
find $\hat{W}$ such that
\begin{equation}
  f_{\rm rec}\!\left(z_{\hat{k}}, \hat{h}, \hat{\dot{h}}, \hat{\alpha},
                \hat{\dot{\alpha}}, \delta_k;\,\theta_r\right) = C_{L,k}^{\rm meas},
  \label{eq:cl_inversion}
\end{equation}
where $f_{\rm rec}$ is evaluated with $W = \hat{W}$ propagated through $f_{\rm dyn}$.
Because $C_L$ is monotonically increasing in $W$ for the operating range
$W \in [0,\,80]\,\text{m/s}$ (a property verified on the LDNet training data),
equation \eqref{eq:cl_inversion} has a unique solution, found by \emph{bisection}
in $O(\log_2(80/0.5)) \approx 7$ LDNet evaluations per step.

\paragraph{Latent state propagation.}
Once $\hat{W}_k$ is available, the latent state is advanced by one step of the
aerodynamic model:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{z}_{k+1} = \hat{z}_k
    + \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta t_{\rm ref}}\,
      f_{\rm dyn}(\hat{z}_k, \hat{h}_k, \hat{\dot{h}}_k,
                  \hat{\alpha}_k, \hat{\dot{\alpha}}_k,
                  \delta_k, \hat{W}_k;\,\theta_d).
  \label{eq:z_propagation}
\end{equation}
This is identical to the plant update \eqref{eq:zdyn} but driven by estimated
rather than true states, so the latent estimate tracks the true $z_k$ as long
as the structural state estimate is accurate.

\paragraph{What the Riccati equation computes.}
The gain $\mK$ is not chosen by trial and error but is the \emph{unique}
solution to an optimisation problem: minimise the infinite-horizon quadratic
cost $J$ in \eqref{eq:cost} subject to the linear dynamics \eqref{eq:aug_sys}.
The necessary condition for optimality is the DARE \eqref{eq:dare}, whose
solution $\mP$ is the \emph{value function} of the problem---the matrix such
that $J^* = \tilde{\vxi}_0^\top \mP\, \tilde{\vxi}_0$ for any initial condition
$\tilde{\vxi}_0$.
Once $\mP$ is known, the optimal gain follows algebraically from
\eqref{eq:gain}.
Intuitively, $\mP$ encodes how costly it is to be in each direction of state
space: a large eigenvalue of $\mP$ along a particular mode means that
deviations in that direction accumulate high future cost and should be corrected
aggressively.

% ================================================================
\subsubsection{Extended Kalman Filter Observer}
\label{sec:ekf}
% ================================================================

The heuristic observer described above is simple and zero-latency, but it
accumulates integration drift in $\hat{h}$ and $\hat{\dot{h}}$ and provides no
principled noise rejection.
An \emph{Extended Kalman Filter} (EKF) is used as a more rigorous alternative:
it propagates the full nonlinear model in the prediction step and applies an
optimal linear correction in the update step.

\paragraph{State and measurements.}
The EKF operates on the same augmented state
$\tilde{\vxi} = [h,\dot{h},\alpha,\dot{\alpha},z,W]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^6$.
The measurement vector is $\mathbf{y} = [\ddot{h},\,\alpha,\,\dot{\alpha}]^\top$,
with output matrix
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{C}_{\rm obs} =
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \mathbf{c}_{\ddot{h}}^\top \\
    \mathbf{e}_3^\top \\
    \mathbf{e}_4^\top
  \end{bmatrix}
  \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times6},
  \label{eq:C_obs}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{e}_3,\mathbf{e}_4$ select $\alpha$ and $\dot{\alpha}$ directly,
and the heave-acceleration row is derived from the linearised structural
equations:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{c}_{\ddot{h}}^\top =
  \frac{1}{\det\mathbf{M}}\Bigl[
    -M_{\alpha\alpha}(K_h,\,D_h,\,-M_{h\alpha}K_\alpha,\,-M_{h\alpha}D_\alpha,\,0)
    - q_\infty(M_{\alpha\alpha}\,\mathbf{c}_{L} - M_{h\alpha}\,\mathbf{c}_{M})
  \Bigr]^\top,
  \label{eq:c_hddot}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{c}_L = \mC_y[0,:]$ and $\mathbf{c}_M = \mC_y[1,:]$ the first and
second rows of the aerodynamic output Jacobian.

\paragraph{Prediction step (nonlinear).}
At each timestep the predicted state is obtained by running the full nonlinear
propagator---LDNet plus the structural RK4 integrator---on the current estimate:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_{k|k-1} = F_{\rm nl}(\hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_{k-1|k-1},\,\delta_{k-1}),
  \label{eq:ekf_pred}
\end{equation}
where $F_{\rm nl}$ is identical to the plant propagator \eqref{eq:propagator}
augmented with the gust decay $W_{k+1} = \lambda_w W_k$.
Using the nonlinear predictor avoids the linearisation error that would arise
from applying $\tilde{\mA}$ when $z$ deviates far from trim during a strong gust.

\paragraph{Update step (linear, $\alpha$ and $\dot{\alpha}$ only).}
The correction uses only the pitch measurements (rows 1--2 of
$\mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}$), because the heave-acceleration row \eqref{eq:c_hddot}
depends on $\mC_y$ evaluated at trim and becomes inaccurate for large $z$
excursions:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_{k|k}
  = \hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_{k|k-1}
  + \mathbf{L}_{1:2}\,
    \bigl(\mathbf{y}_{1:2,k} - \mathbf{C}_{\rm obs,1:2}\,\hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_{k|k-1}\bigr),
  \label{eq:ekf_update}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{y}_{1:2} = [\alpha,\dot{\alpha}]^\top$ and
$\mathbf{L}_{1:2} \in \mathbb{R}^{6\times2}$ are the corresponding columns of
the Kalman gain matrix $\mathbf{L}$.
The $W$ row of $\mathbf{L}$ is zeroed: the gust estimate is always set by
$C_L$ bisection \eqref{eq:cl_inversion}, which is more reliable than the
filter's $W$ correction when $C_y$ is evaluated far from trim.

\paragraph{Kalman gain computation (dual DARE).}
The gain $\mathbf{L}$ is computed once offline by solving the \emph{dual} of
the DARE \eqref{eq:dare}---the observer Riccati equation:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{S} = \mathbf{Q}_{\rm kf}
    + \tilde{\mA}\,\mathbf{S}\,\tilde{\mA}^\top
    - \tilde{\mA}\,\mathbf{S}\,\mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}^\top
      \bigl(\mathbf{R}_{\rm kf} + \mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}\,\mathbf{S}\,\mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}^\top\bigr)^{-1}
      \mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}\,\mathbf{S}\,\tilde{\mA}^\top,
  \label{eq:dare_obs}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{Q}_{\rm kf} = \operatorname{diag}(10^{-6},10^{-6},10^{-6},10^{-6},10^{-8},0.1)$
is the process noise covariance and
$\mathbf{R}_{\rm kf} = \operatorname{diag}((10^{-2})^2,(10^{-3})^2,(5\times10^{-3})^2)$
is the measurement noise covariance (accelerometer, AoA encoder, AoA rate).
The steady-state gain is then
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{L} = \mathbf{S}\,\mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}^\top
    \bigl(\mathbf{R}_{\rm kf} + \mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}\,\mathbf{S}\,\mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}^\top\bigr)^{-1}
  \in \mathbb{R}^{6\times3}.
  \label{eq:kalman_gain}
\end{equation}
The dual DARE \eqref{eq:dare_obs} is the transpose of \eqref{eq:dare} with
$(\tilde{\mA}, \tilde{\mB}, \tilde{\mQ})$ replaced by
$(\tilde{\mA}^\top, \mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}^\top, \mathbf{Q}_{\rm kf})$,
so the same \texttt{scipy.linalg.solve\_discrete\_are} solver and Schur
decomposition apply.

\subsubsection{Aeroelastic Model and Trim Condition}
\label{sec:model}
% ================================================================

\subsubsection{Structural Dynamics}

The wing is modelled as a rigid section with two degrees of freedom: heave
displacement $h$ (positive downward) and pitch angle $\alpha$ (positive
nose-up).  Aerodynamic forces are transmitted to the structure via a trailing-edge
flap with deflection angle $\delta$.

The equations of motion in coupled form are
\begin{equation}
  \begin{bmatrix} M_{hh} & M_{h\alpha} \\ M_{h\alpha} & M_{\alpha\alpha} \end{bmatrix}
  \begin{bmatrix} \ddot{h} \\ \ddot{\alpha} \end{bmatrix}
  =
  \begin{bmatrix} -F_y - D_h\dot{h} - K_h h \\ M_z - D_\alpha\dot{\alpha} - K_\alpha\alpha \end{bmatrix},
  \label{eq:eom}
\end{equation}
where $F_y = q_\infty C_L$ and $M_z = q_\infty C_M$ are the aerodynamic lift
force and pitching moment, $q_\infty = \tfrac{1}{2}\rho U_\infty^2 S$ is the
dynamic pressure ($S = 0.05\,\text{m}^2$, $\rho = 1.225\,\text{kg/m}^3$), and
the inertial coupling arises from the off-centre flap mass.

\subsubsection{Neural Aerodynamic Surrogate (LDNet)}

The aerodynamic coefficients $C_L$ and $C_M$ are produced by a recurrent
neural network with a scalar latent state $z \in \mathbb{R}$:
\begin{align}
  z_{k+1} &= z_k + \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta t_{\rm ref}}\,f_{\rm dyn}(z_k, h_k, \dot{h}_k, \alpha_k, \dot{\alpha}_k, \delta_k, W_k;\,\theta_d),
  \label{eq:zdyn}\\
  \begin{bmatrix} C_L \\ C_M \end{bmatrix}_k &= f_{\rm rec}(z_k, h_k, \dot{h}_k, \alpha_k, \dot{\alpha}_k, \delta_k;\,\theta_r),
  \label{eq:yrec}
\end{align}
where $W_k$ is the gust velocity, $f_{\rm dyn}$ is a shallow MLP (7-7-1), and
$f_{\rm rec}$ is a deeper network (24-24-24-24-2).
Both networks are kept frozen during control design.

The full state is the concatenation of structural and latent variables:
\begin{equation}
  \vxi_k = \begin{bmatrix} h_k & \dot{h}_k & \alpha_k & \dot{\alpha}_k & z_k \end{bmatrix}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^5.
\end{equation}
The trim condition is defined at free-stream velocity $U_\infty = 75\,\text{m/s}$
with zero gust ($W = 0$) and zero structural displacement.
The latent state $z_{\rm tr}$ is obtained by iterating \eqref{eq:zdyn} to
steady state with $\delta = 0$; at trim $C_{L,\rm tr} \approx 0.031$ and
$C_{M,\rm tr} \approx 0$.
The trim point is therefore
\begin{equation}
  \vxi_{\rm tr} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & z_{\rm tr} \end{bmatrix}^\top, \quad
  u_{\rm tr} = \delta_{\rm tr} = 0.
\end{equation}

\section{Jacobian Linearisation via Automatic Differentiation}
\label{sec:jacobian}
% ================================================================

\subsubsection{Discrete-Time Propagator}

The combined structural integrator and aerodynamic network define a
discrete-time propagator
\begin{equation}
  \vxi_{k+1} = F(\vxi_k, u_k),
  \label{eq:propagator}
\end{equation}
where $F: \mathbb{R}^5 \times \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^5$ is evaluated in
two stages:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Aerodynamic step.}  Given $(\vxi_k, u_k)$, evaluate
        LDNet to obtain $z_{k+1}$, $C_L$, $C_M$ via
        \eqref{eq:zdyn}--\eqref{eq:yrec} with $W = 0$ (no gust at trim).
  \item \textbf{Structural step.}  Integrate \eqref{eq:eom} over one timestep
        $\Delta t = 0.01\,\text{s}$ using a fourth-order Runge--Kutta (RK4)
        scheme with the aerodynamic forcing fixed at the values from step~1:
        \begin{align}
          \dot{\bm{x}} &= \bm{g}(\bm{x}, F_y, M_z), \nonumber\\
          g_1 &= \dot{h},\quad
          g_2 = \frac{M_{\alpha\alpha}(-F_y - D_h\dot{h} - K_h h)
                      - M_{h\alpha}(M_z - D_\alpha\dot{\alpha} - K_\alpha\alpha)}
                     {M_{hh}M_{\alpha\alpha} - M_{h\alpha}^2},\nonumber\\
          g_3 &= \dot{\alpha},\quad
          g_4 = \frac{M_{hh}(M_z - D_\alpha\dot{\alpha} - K_\alpha\alpha)
                      - M_{h\alpha}(-F_y - D_h\dot{h} - K_h h)}
                     {M_{hh}M_{\alpha\alpha} - M_{h\alpha}^2},
          \label{eq:rhs}
        \end{align}
        where $\bm{x} = [h,\dot{h},\alpha,\dot{\alpha}]^\top$ and the
        determinant $\det \mathbf{M} = M_{hh}M_{\alpha\alpha} - M_{h\alpha}^2$.
\end{enumerate}
The output equation evaluated at the same step is
\begin{equation}
  \vy_k = \begin{bmatrix} C_L \\ C_M \end{bmatrix}_k = g(\vxi_k, u_k).
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Five Jacobian Matrices}

Linearising \eqref{eq:propagator} and the output map around the trim point
$(\vxi_{\rm tr}, 0)$, and including the sensitivity to the gust velocity $W$,
yields the discrete-time LTI system
\begin{equation}
  \delta\vxi_{k+1} = \mA_d\,\delta\vxi_k + \mB_d\,\delta u_k + \mathbf{b}_w\,W_k, \qquad
  \delta\vy_k = \mC_y\,\delta\vxi_k + \mD_y\,\delta u_k,
  \label{eq:lti}
\end{equation}
where $\delta\vxi = \vxi - \vxi_{\rm tr}$, $\delta u = u - u_{\rm tr}$, and
\begin{equation}
  \mA_d = \frac{\partial F}{\partial \vxi}\bigg|_{\rm tr} \in \mathbb{R}^{5\times5},\quad
  \mB_d = \frac{\partial F}{\partial u}\bigg|_{\rm tr} \in \mathbb{R}^{5\times1},\quad
  \mathbf{b}_w = \frac{\partial F}{\partial W}\bigg|_{\rm tr} \in \mathbb{R}^{5},
  \label{eq:jacobians}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \mC_y = \frac{\partial g}{\partial \vxi}\bigg|_{\rm tr} \in \mathbb{R}^{2\times5},\quad
  \mD_y = \frac{\partial g}{\partial u}\bigg|_{\rm tr} \in \mathbb{R}^{2\times1}.
  \label{eq:jacobians_out}
\end{equation}
The gust-input vector $\mathbf{b}_w$ is obtained by declaring $W$ as an additional
\texttt{tf.Variable} inside the same tape and calling \texttt{tape.jacobian}
on $\vxi_{k+1}$ with respect to it, at no extra computational cost.


\subsubsection{Computation via Reverse-Mode Automatic Differentiation}

Because $F$ passes through the LDNet weights, closed-form expressions for the
partial derivatives in \eqref{eq:jacobians} are not available.
All five matrices are instead obtained analytically---without finite
differences---by \emph{reverse-mode automatic differentiation} (AD) applied to
the full discrete-time map
\begin{equation}
  \Phi : (\vxi,\, u,\, W) \;\longmapsto\;
  \bigl[\,F(\vxi, u, W)^\top,\;\; g(\vxi, u)^\top\,\bigr]^\top
  \in \mathbb{R}^{7},
  \label{eq:Phi}
\end{equation}
evaluated at the trim point $(\vxi_{\rm tr}, 0, 0)$.

\paragraph{Decomposition into elementary operations.}
Both the RK4 integrator \eqref{eq:rhs} and the two neural-network layers
$f_{\rm dyn}$, $f_{\rm rec}$ are finite compositions of differentiable
primitive operations (affine maps, element-wise activations, additions).
The full map $\Phi$ can therefore be written as
\begin{equation}
  \Phi(\vxi, u, W)
  = \phi_K \circ \phi_{K-1} \circ \cdots \circ \phi_1(\vxi, u, W),
  \label{eq:chain}
\end{equation}
where each $\phi_k : \mathbb{R}^{n_k} \to \mathbb{R}^{n_{k+1}}$ is one of two
types of primitive operation whose Jacobian is trivial by construction:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Affine layer.} $\phi_k(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{W}_k\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}_k$
        is linear, so $\mathbf{J}_k = \mathbf{W}_k$ exactly---the weight matrix
        already stored in memory, evaluated at zero cost.
  \item \textbf{Element-wise activation.} $\phi_k(\mathbf{x}) = \sigma(\mathbf{x})$
        applied component-by-component gives a diagonal Jacobian
        $\mathbf{J}_k = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma'(x_1),\ldots,\sigma'(x_n))$,
        where $\sigma'$ is the scalar derivative of the activation function
        (e.g.\ $\sigma'(x) = 1 - \tanh^2(x)$ for a $\tanh$ unit,
        already computed during the forward pass).
\end{itemize}
No global differentiation of $F$ or $g$ is required: the chain rule assembles
these local primitives automatically.

\paragraph{Forward pass and adjoint back-propagation.}
To compute the $i$-th row of the Jacobian $\partial\Phi/\partial\vxi$,
reverse-mode AD performs a single \emph{forward pass} to record the
intermediate values $\phi_1, \ldots, \phi_K$, then a single \emph{backward
pass} that accumulates the adjoint vector
$\bar{\mathbf{v}} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_k}$ from output to input via the
transpose chain rule:
\begin{equation}
  \bar{\mathbf{v}}_{k-1}
  = \mathbf{J}_k^\top\,\bar{\mathbf{v}}_k,
  \qquad
  \bar{\mathbf{v}}_K = \mathbf{e}_i,
  \label{eq:adjoint}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{e}_i$ is the $i$-th standard basis vector.
After $K$ steps, $\bar{\mathbf{v}}_0 = \partial\Phi_i/\partial\vxi$.
Repeating for each of the 7 output components of $\Phi$ yields all five
Jacobian matrices in \eqref{eq:jacobians}--\eqref{eq:jacobians_out} exactly,
at a cost of 7 backward passes through the same computational graph---far
cheaper than $5+1+1 = 7$ forward finite-difference perturbations and with
no truncation error.

\paragraph{Exact propagation through RK4.}
A subtlety arises because $F$ is itself a four-stage composition (the RK4
sub-steps $k_1, \ldots, k_4$), each of which calls LDNet.
Reverse-mode AD treats each sub-step as an additional set of primitive
operations $\phi_k$ in \eqref{eq:chain}, so the chain rule \eqref{eq:adjoint}
propagates sensitivities \emph{exactly} through all four stages and through
the neural-network activations, with no additional approximation.

The open-loop eigenvalues $\lambda_i(\mA_d)$ all satisfy $|\lambda_i| < 1$,
confirming that the trim equilibrium is asymptotically stable in the
linearised sense, albeit with low modal damping
($|\lambda_i| \approx 0.97$--$0.99$ for the aeroelastic modes).

\subsubsection{LQR Formulation and Discrete Algebraic Riccati Equation}
\label{sec:lqr}
% ================================================================

\subsubsection{Gust-Augmented State and Cost Function}

To exploit the estimated gust velocity $\hat{W}_k$ as a feed-forward signal,
the state vector is extended with a gust state.  A first-order exponential
decay is assumed as the gust model:
\begin{equation}
  W_{k+1} = \lambda_w\, W_k, \qquad \lambda_w = 0.98,
  \label{eq:gust_model}
\end{equation}
giving the augmented state $\tilde{\vxi}_k = [\vxi_k^\top,\, W_k]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^6$
and the augmented system
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{\vxi}_{k+1}
  =
  \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} \mA_d & \mathbf{b}_w \\ \mathbf{0}^\top & \lambda_w \end{bmatrix}}_{\tilde{\mA} \in \mathbb{R}^{6\times6}}
  \tilde{\vxi}_k
  +
  \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} \mB_d \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}}_{\tilde{\mB} \in \mathbb{R}^{6\times1}}
  \delta_k.
  \label{eq:aug_sys}
\end{equation}
The gust-input column $\mathbf{b}_w$ (computed in \eqref{eq:jacobians}) appears
in the top-right block of $\tilde{\mA}$, so the DARE automatically infers the
optimal feed-forward gain on the estimated gust.

The infinite-horizon cost over the augmented state is
\begin{equation}
  J = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
      \Bigl[\tilde{\vxi}_k^\top \tilde{\mQ}\,\tilde{\vxi}_k
           + \delta u_k^\top \mR_{\rm aug}\,\delta u_k\Bigr],
  \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{\mQ} \in \mathbb{R}^{6\times6}$ is block-diagonal:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{\mQ} = \begin{bmatrix} \mQ_{\rm aug} & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0}^\top & Q_w \end{bmatrix},
  \qquad
  \mQ_{\rm aug} = \mQ + \mC_{y,6}^\top \mQ_y \mC_{y,6},
  \quad
  \mR_{\rm aug} = \mR + \mD_y^\top \mQ_y \mD_y,
  \label{eq:augmented}
\end{equation}
with $\mC_{y,6} = [\mC_y\;\mathbf{0}] \in \mathbb{R}^{2\times6}$ (the output
Jacobian padded with a zero column for the W state, since $W$ does not appear
directly in $f_{\rm rec}$), and $Q_w \ge 0$ an optional penalty on the gust state.

The role of $\mQ_y$ becomes transparent by expanding the state cost:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{\vxi}_k^\top \mQ_{\rm aug}\, \tilde{\vxi}_k
  = \tilde{\vxi}_k^\top \mQ\, \tilde{\vxi}_k
  + \underbrace{(\mC_{y,6}\,\tilde{\vxi}_k)^\top \mQ_y\, (\mC_{y,6}\,\tilde{\vxi}_k)}_{%
      \approx\,\delta\vy_k^\top \mQ_y\,\delta\vy_k
      \,=\, Q_{y,L}\,\Delta C_L^2 + Q_{y,M}\,\Delta C_M^2},
  \label{eq:cost_expand}
\end{equation}
where $\delta\vy_k = \mC_{y,6}\,\tilde{\vxi}_k \approx [C_{L,k} - C_{L,\rm tr},\; C_{M,k} - C_{M,\rm tr}]^\top$
is the linearised aerodynamic deviation from trim.
Penalising $\tilde{\vxi}$ with $\mC_{y,6}^\top \mQ_y \mC_{y,6}$ is therefore
equivalent (to first order) to penalising $C_L$ and $C_M$ deviations directly,
without augmenting the state vector.

The weight values used in this work are
\begin{equation}
  \mQ = \operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{1}{0.00428^2},\,0,\,\frac{5}{0.00823^2},\,0,\,0\right),
  \quad
  \mR = \left[\frac{1}{0.5^2}\right],
  \label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
normalised by the expected peak amplitudes of $h$ and $\alpha$ under a
$60\,\text{m/s}$ gust.

\paragraph{Why $\mQ_y = 0$ in the baseline.}
Setting $\mQ_y = 0$ does \emph{not} mean that $C_L$ and $C_M$ are ignored.
The state penalty $\mQ$ already penalises $h$ and $\alpha$, and since
$C_L \approx C_{L,\rm tr} + \mC_y\delta\vxi$ (from the output Jacobian),
reducing structural oscillations indirectly attenuates aerodynamic excursions.
The output penalty $\mQ_y$ adds a \emph{direct} cost on $\Delta C_L$ and
$\Delta C_M$, independently of whether they are caused by structural motion or
by the gust acting on a near-stationary wing.
It becomes useful when the aerodynamic loads matter in their own right
(e.g., fatigue or certification limits on $C_M$), at the price of a larger
flap effort; this trade-off is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}.
No gust penalty ($Q_w = 0$) is applied either, meaning the DARE optimises the
feed-forward gain $K_w$ purely through the coupling introduced by $\mathbf{b}_w$
in $\tilde{\mA}$.

\subsubsection{Discrete Algebraic Riccati Equation}

The solution to the infinite-horizon LQR problem for the augmented system
\eqref{eq:aug_sys} is the unique positive semi-definite solution $\mP$ of the
$6\times6$ \emph{Discrete Algebraic Riccati Equation} (DARE):
\begin{equation}
  \mP = \tilde{\mQ}
        + \tilde{\mA}^\top \mP \tilde{\mA}
        - \tilde{\mA}^\top \mP \tilde{\mB}
          \bigl(\mR_{\rm aug} + \tilde{\mB}^\top \mP \tilde{\mB}\bigr)^{-1}
          \tilde{\mB}^\top \mP \tilde{\mA},
  \label{eq:dare}
\end{equation}
Because \eqref{eq:dare} is a fixed-point equation quadratic in $\mP$, it cannot
be solved by direct inversion.
\texttt{scipy.linalg.solve\_discrete\_are} \cite{anderson1990optimal} finds the
unique stabilising solution via the \emph{Schur decomposition} of the
$12\times12$ symplectic pencil
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{H} =
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \tilde{\mA} & \tilde{\mB}\mR_{\rm aug}^{-1}\tilde{\mB}^\top \\
    -\tilde{\mQ} & -\tilde{\mA}^{-\top}
  \end{bmatrix}\!.
  \label{eq:symplectic}
\end{equation}
The $n=6$ Schur vectors associated with eigenvalues inside the unit disk span
the stable invariant subspace of $\mathbf{H}$; partitioning them as
$[U_1^\top,\, U_2^\top]^\top$ gives $\mP = U_2 U_1^{-1}$ in a finite number
of $\mathcal{O}(n^3)$ floating-point operations, with no iteration required.
For the $6\times6$ system considered here the solve is instantaneous and
performed once at design time.

\subsection{Optimal Gain and Control Law}

The optimal gain partitions naturally into a state-feedback component and a
gust feed-forward component:
\begin{equation}
  \mK = \bigl(\mR_{\rm aug} + \tilde{\mB}^\top \mP \tilde{\mB}\bigr)^{-1}
        \tilde{\mB}^\top \mP \tilde{\mA}
        = \bigl[\underbrace{\mK_\xi}_{1\times5} \;\big|\; \underbrace{K_w}_{1\times1}\bigr]
        \in \mathbb{R}^{1\times6},
  \label{eq:gain}
\end{equation}
and the control law at each timestep is
\begin{equation}
  \delta_t = -\mK_\xi\,(\hat{\vxi}_t - \vxi_{\rm tr}) - K_w\,\hat{W}_t,\qquad
  \delta_t \in [-20^\circ,\,+20^\circ],
  \label{eq:law}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{\vxi}_t$ is the estimated structural+latent state and $\hat{W}_t$
is the estimated gust velocity from the observer.
The closed-loop matrix $\tilde{\mA} - \tilde{\mB}\mK$ has all eigenvalues
strictly inside the unit circle, confirming closed-loop stability.

The entire gain $\mK$ is computed once offline; at runtime \eqref{eq:law}
requires only a single $1\times6$ matrix-vector product and a scalar
clip---negligible cost compared to the online MPC optimisation.

\subsubsection{Setup}

Four configurations are compared under a single 1-cosine gust
\begin{equation}
  W(t) = \frac{W_0}{2}\Bigl(1 - \cos\tfrac{2\pi t}{T_g}\Bigr),
  \quad 0 \le t \le T_g,
\end{equation}
with $W_0 = 60\,\text{m/s}$, $T_g = 1\,\text{s}$, $U_\infty = 75\,\text{m/s}$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Baseline} --- open-loop, no flap actuation.
  \item \textbf{LQR + true state} --- oracle: controller fed the exact plant
        state $\tilde{\vxi}_k$ at each step.  Upper bound on achievable performance.
  \item \textbf{LQR + heuristic} --- leaky integrator + $C_L$ bisection observer
        (Section~\ref{sec:lqr_arch}).
  \item \textbf{LQR + EKF} --- Extended Kalman Filter observer
        (Section~\ref{sec:ekf}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Sensitivity to Q/R Weights and Design Trade-offs}
\label{sec:sensitivity}
% ================================================================

The LQR design involves three tuning matrices: $\mQ$ (state cost), $\mR$
(control cost), and optionally $\mQ_y$ (output penalty).  Their influence on
closed-loop behaviour follows directly from the structure of the DARE solution.

\paragraph{Effect of $\mQ$ and $\mQ_y$.}
Increasing the heave weight $Q_h$ or output penalty $Q_y$ causes the Riccati
solution $\mP$ to grow, which in turn increases the magnitude of $\mK$ via
\eqref{eq:gain}.  A larger gain produces faster disturbance rejection but
requires higher flap deflections.  In the extreme case where $Q_h \gg R$, the
controller approaches a minimum-time regulator and $\delta$ may saturate the
$\pm 20^\circ$ limit, rendering the linear approximation invalid.

\paragraph{Effect of $\mR$.}
Increasing $\mR$ penalises control effort directly.  The gain $\mK$ decreases,
resulting in slower but smoother actuation.  For the present system the ratio
$Q_h / R$ is the dominant design parameter: at the baseline value
$Q_h/R \approx 54596 / 4 \approx 13649$, the controller achieves good
oscillation attenuation without saturating the actuator.

\paragraph{Output penalty $\mQ_y$.}
Setting $\mQ_y > 0$ shifts the cost from state excursion to aerodynamic
output deviation.  Because the output Jacobian $\mC_y$ couples all five states
to $C_L$ and $C_M$, the augmented weight $\mQ_{\rm aug}$ becomes full even when
$\mQ$ is diagonal.  This can improve $C_M$ attenuation at the cost of larger
heave excursions, trading structural load metrics against structural
displacement metrics.

\paragraph{Implicit sensitivity.}
The gain $\mK(\mQ,\mR)$ is an implicit function of the weight matrices through
the DARE solution.  No closed-form sensitivity expression exists; the
dependence must be evaluated numerically.  For extreme weight ratios
($Q/R > 10^6$ or $Q/R < 10^{-2}$), the DARE may become ill-conditioned,
and the solver may fail to converge.

\subsubsection{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}
% ================================================================

This chapter has presented an LQR controller for gust load alleviation on the
LDNet aeroelastic model.  The key contributions are:

\begin{itemize}
  \item A linearisation procedure based on automatic differentiation that
        obtains exact Jacobians of a neural-network plant without manual
        derivation.
  \item An output-augmented LQR formulation that penalises aerodynamic
        coefficient deviations at trim using the output Jacobians
        $\mC_y$ and $\mD_y$.
  \item A gust-augmented state formulation that appends the estimated gust
        velocity $\hat{W}$ as a sixth state, with gust-input column
        $\mathbf{b}_w = \partial F/\partial W|_{\rm tr}$ computed by the same
        automatic differentiation pass.  The DARE then yields an optimal
        feed-forward gain $K_w$ on the gust estimate at no extra design effort.
  \item Demonstration that a single offline $6\times6$ DARE solve provides a
        real-time-capable gain (one $1\times6$ matrix-vector product per timestep)
        with meaningful structural load reduction under a $60\,\text{m/s}$
        1-cosine gust.
\end{itemize}

The primary limitation of the LQR approach is its reactive nature: without
preview information about the incoming gust, the initial aerodynamic peak
at $t \approx 0.3\,\text{s}$ cannot be avoided.  MPC, by rolling out an
explicit nonlinear horizon, achieves further reduction of this first peak
at significantly higher computational cost.  The LQR presented here represents
a practical baseline for real-time implementation where computational resources
are constrained.
