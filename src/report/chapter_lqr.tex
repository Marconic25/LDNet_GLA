\subsection{Closed-Loop Results with LQR}
% ================================================================

The primary objective of gust load alleviation (GLA) is to reduce the peak
lift coefficient $C_L$ that arises when a 1-cosine gust strikes the wing.
The $C_L$ spike drives aerodynamic loads and, through the structural equations
of motion, excites heave and pitch oscillations; attenuating it at the source
therefore suppresses both aerodynamic and structural responses simultaneously.
The control actuator is a single trailing-edge flap with deflection $\delta$,
commanded in real time.

Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) is chosen as the control strategy.
Compared with Model Predictive Control (MPC), which solves a constrained
nonlinear optimisation at every timestep, LQR computes the optimal feedback
gain $\mK$ \emph{offline once} by solving a Discrete Algebraic Riccati Equation
(DARE); the online computational cost is then a single matrix--vector product.
Optimality is guaranteed in the neighbourhood of the linearisation point (trim),
which is sufficient for the moderate gust intensities considered here.

The plant is the \emph{LDNet} model---a data-driven aeroelastic emulator that
couples a two-degree-of-freedom (2-DOF) structural wing with a neural-network
aerodynamic surrogate.
Because the aerodynamic model is a trained neural network, closed-form Jacobians
of the discrete-time propagator are unavailable; they are obtained exactly via
\emph{automatic differentiation} (AD) of the full propagator, with no finite
differences and no truncation error.
These Jacobians feed directly into the DARE to yield the optimal gain and, via
the dual Riccati equation, the steady-state Kalman gain for the observer.

The rest of this section is organised as follows.
Section~\ref{sec:model} introduces the plant and trim condition.
Section~\ref{sec:jacobian} derives the Jacobian linearisation via AD.
Section~\ref{sec:lqr} formulates the cost function---with $C_L$ as the primary
target---and solves the DARE for the optimal gain $\mK$.
Section~\ref{sec:lqr_arch} presents the block diagram and the per-timestep
online loop that assembles these offline components.
Section~\ref{sec:ekf} details the Extended Kalman Filter observer.
Section~\ref{sec:results} presents closed-loop simulation results.

\subsubsection{Aeroelastic Model and Trim Condition}
\label{sec:model}
% ================================================================

\paragraph{Structural dynamics.}
The wing is modelled as a rigid section with two degrees of freedom: heave
displacement $h$ (positive downward) and pitch angle $\alpha$ (positive
nose-up).
Aerodynamic forces are transmitted to the structure via a trailing-edge flap
with deflection angle $\delta$.
The equations of motion in coupled form are
\begin{equation}
  \begin{bmatrix} M_{hh} & M_{h\alpha} \\ M_{h\alpha} & M_{\alpha\alpha} \end{bmatrix}
  \begin{bmatrix} \ddot{h} \\ \ddot{\alpha} \end{bmatrix}
  =
  \begin{bmatrix} -F_y - D_h\dot{h} - K_h h \\ M_z - D_\alpha\dot{\alpha} - K_\alpha\alpha \end{bmatrix},
  \label{eq:eom}
\end{equation}
where $F_y = q_\infty C_L$ and $M_z = q_\infty C_M$ are the aerodynamic lift
force and pitching moment, $q_\infty = \tfrac{1}{2}\rho U_\infty^2 S$ is the
dynamic pressure ($S = 0.05\,\text{m}^2$, $\rho = 1.225\,\text{kg/m}^3$), and
the inertial coupling arises from the off-centre flap mass.

\paragraph{Neural aerodynamic surrogate (LDNet).}
The aerodynamic coefficients $C_L$ and $C_M$ are produced by a recurrent
neural network with a scalar latent state $z \in \mathbb{R}$:
\begin{align}
  z_{k+1} &= z_k + \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta t_{\rm ref}}\,
             f_{\rm dyn}(z_k, h_k, \dot{h}_k, \alpha_k, \dot{\alpha}_k,
                         \delta_k, W_k;\,\theta_d),
  \label{eq:zdyn}\\
  \begin{bmatrix} C_L \\ C_M \end{bmatrix}_k
             &= f_{\rm rec}(z_k, h_k, \dot{h}_k, \alpha_k, \dot{\alpha}_k,
                            \delta_k;\,\theta_r),
  \label{eq:yrec}
\end{align}
where $W_k$ is the gust velocity, $f_{\rm dyn}$ is a shallow MLP (7-7-1), and
$f_{\rm rec}$ is a deeper network (24-24-24-24-2).
Both networks are kept frozen during control design.
The full state concatenates structural and latent variables:
\begin{equation}
  \vxi_k = \bigl[h_k,\;\dot{h}_k,\;\alpha_k,\;\dot{\alpha}_k,\;z_k\bigr]^\top
  \in \mathbb{R}^5.
\end{equation}
The combined structural integrator and aerodynamic network define the
discrete-time propagator
\begin{equation}
  \vxi_{k+1} = F(\vxi_k,\, u_k),
  \label{eq:propagator}
\end{equation}
where $F$ advances $z$ via $f_{\rm dyn}$ and the structural state via a
fourth-order Runge--Kutta (RK4) scheme driven by the aerodynamic forces from
$f_{\rm rec}$.

\paragraph{Trim condition.}
The trim point is defined at $U_\infty = 75\,\text{m/s}$, zero gust, and zero
structural displacement: $\vxi_{\rm tr} = [0,0,0,0,z_{\rm tr}]^\top$,
$\delta_{\rm tr} = 0$.
The latent state $z_{\rm tr}$ is obtained by running 200 warm-up steps of
\eqref{eq:zdyn} from $z_0 = 0$ with all inputs zero, yielding
$z_{\rm tr} \approx -0.46$ and $C_{L,\rm tr} \approx 0.035$,
$C_{M,\rm tr} \approx 0.031$.
No exact fixed point exists for this network (the residual of a
Levenberg--Marquardt solve is $\approx 0.8$), so the warm-up value is used as
the practical trim; the resulting control bias is $|\Delta\delta| \lesssim 0.6^\circ$
($3\%$ of the $\pm20^\circ$ actuator range) and has negligible effect on
closed-loop performance.

\subsubsection{Jacobian Linearisation via Automatic Differentiation}
\label{sec:jacobian}
% ================================================================

Linearising \eqref{eq:propagator} and the aerodynamic output map around the
trim point $(\vxi_{\rm tr}, 0)$, and including the sensitivity to the gust
velocity $W$, yields the discrete-time LTI system
\begin{equation}
  \delta\vxi_{k+1} = \mA_d\,\delta\vxi_k + \mB_d\,\delta u_k + \mathbf{b}_w\,W_k,
  \qquad
  \delta\vy_k = \mC_y\,\delta\vxi_k + \mD_y\,\delta u_k,
  \label{eq:lti}
\end{equation}
where $\delta\vxi = \vxi - \vxi_{\rm tr}$, $\delta u = u - u_{\rm tr}$,
and $g(\vxi, u) = [C_L, C_M]^\top$ is the aerodynamic output map \eqref{eq:yrec}
(a byproduct of the propagator \eqref{eq:propagator}, computed at no extra cost).
Here $\delta\vy_k = [C_{L,k} - C_{L,\rm tr},\; C_{M,k} - C_{M,\rm tr}]^\top
\in \mathbb{R}^2$ is a \emph{performance output}---the quantity the LQR cost
penalises---not a measurement output: it is never fed to the observer, which
uses the physical sensor vector $\mathbf{y}_k = [\ddot{h}_k, \alpha_k]^\top$
\eqref{eq:meas_vec} instead.
The five Jacobian matrices are
\begin{equation}
  \mA_d = \frac{\partial F}{\partial \vxi}\bigg|_{\rm tr} \in \mathbb{R}^{5\times5},\quad
  \mB_d = \frac{\partial F}{\partial u}\bigg|_{\rm tr} \in \mathbb{R}^{5\times1},\quad
  \mathbf{b}_w = \frac{\partial F}{\partial W}\bigg|_{\rm tr} \in \mathbb{R}^{5},
  \label{eq:jacobians}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
  \mC_y = \frac{\partial g}{\partial \vxi}\bigg|_{\rm tr} \in \mathbb{R}^{2\times5},\quad
  \mD_y = \frac{\partial g}{\partial u}\bigg|_{\rm tr} \in \mathbb{R}^{2\times1}.
  \label{eq:jacobians_out}
\end{equation}
Because $F$ passes through the LDNet weights, closed-form expressions for these
partial derivatives are unavailable.
All five matrices are instead obtained analytically---without finite
differences---by \emph{reverse-mode automatic differentiation} (AD) applied to
the full discrete-time map
\begin{equation}
  \Phi : (\vxi,\, u,\, W) \;\longmapsto\;
  \bigl[\,F(\vxi, u, W)^\top,\;\; g(\vxi, u)^\top\,\bigr]^\top
  \in \mathbb{R}^{7},
  \label{eq:Phi}
\end{equation}
evaluated at the trim point.
Stacking state and output maps into $\Phi \in \mathbb{R}^7$ allows all five
Jacobian matrices to be obtained in a single AD call.

Both the RK4 integrator and the two neural-network sub-networks ($f_{\rm dyn}$,
$f_{\rm rec}$) are finite compositions of differentiable primitives---affine
layers and element-wise activations---so $\Phi$ can be written as
\begin{equation}
  \Phi(\vxi, u, W)
  = \phi_K \circ \phi_{K-1} \circ \cdots \circ \phi_1(\vxi, u, W).
  \label{eq:chain}
\end{equation}
Each primitive $\phi_k$ has a trivially computable Jacobian:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Affine layer.}
        $\phi_k(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{W}_k\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}_k$,\quad
        $\mathbf{J}_k = \mathbf{W}_k$
        (the weight matrix already stored in memory, evaluated at zero cost).
  \item \textbf{Element-wise activation.}
        $\phi_k(\mathbf{x}) = \sigma(\mathbf{x})$ applied component-by-component,\quad
        $\mathbf{J}_k = \operatorname{diag}\!\bigl(\sigma'(x_1),\ldots,\sigma'(x_n)\bigr)$
        (e.g.\ $\sigma'(x)=1-\tanh^2(x)$ for a $\tanh$ unit, already computed
        during the forward pass).
\end{itemize}
The right-hand side $\bm{g}$ of the structural ODE is linear in the structural
state, so RK4 sub-steps are affine operations in the AD graph and require no
special treatment.
To compute the $i$-th row of the full Jacobian, reverse-mode AD performs one
forward pass to record the computational graph and one backward pass that
accumulates the adjoint vector via the transpose chain rule:
\begin{equation}
  \bar{\mathbf{v}}_{k-1} = \mathbf{J}_k^\top\,\bar{\mathbf{v}}_k,
  \qquad \bar{\mathbf{v}}_K = \mathbf{e}_i.
  \label{eq:adjoint}
\end{equation}
Repeating for each of the 7 outputs of $\Phi$ yields all five matrices exactly,
at the cost of 7 backward passes---with no truncation error, unlike finite
differences.
The gust-input vector $\mathbf{b}_w$ is obtained by treating $W$ as an
additional \texttt{tf.Variable} inside the same tape, at no extra cost.

The open-loop eigenvalues $\lambda_i(\mA_d)$ all satisfy $|\lambda_i| < 1$,
confirming asymptotic stability at trim, albeit with low modal damping
($|\lambda_i| \approx 0.97$--$0.99$ for the aeroelastic modes).

\subsubsection{LQR Design: Cost Function, Augmented State, and DARE}
\label{sec:lqr}
% ================================================================

\paragraph{Primary objective: $C_L$ peak reduction.}
The cost function is designed so that the $C_L$ peak is the primary penalised
quantity.
By expressing the aerodynamic deviation from trim as
$\delta\vy_k \approx \mC_y\,\delta\vxi_k$,
penalising $\delta\vxi$ with the matrix $\mC_y^\top \mQ_y \mC_y$ is equivalent
(to first order) to penalising $\Delta C_L^2$ and $\Delta C_M^2$ directly.
The structural states $h$ and $\alpha$ are penalised separately, so their
oscillations are suppressed as a secondary objective.

\paragraph{Gust-augmented state and system.}
To exploit the estimated gust velocity $\hat{W}_k$ as a feed-forward signal,
the state vector is extended with a gust state governed by a first-order
exponential decay:
\begin{equation}
  W_{k+1} = \lambda_w\, W_k, \qquad \lambda_w = 0.98,
  \label{eq:gust_model}
\end{equation}
giving the augmented state $\tilde{\vxi}_k = [\vxi_k^\top,\, W_k]^\top
\in \mathbb{R}^6$ and the augmented system
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{\vxi}_{k+1}
  =
  \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} \mA_d & \mathbf{b}_w \\ \mathbf{0}^\top & \lambda_w \end{bmatrix}}_{\tilde{\mA} \in \mathbb{R}^{6\times6}}
  \tilde{\vxi}_k
  +
  \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} \mB_d \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}}_{\tilde{\mB} \in \mathbb{R}^{6\times1}}
  \delta_k.
  \label{eq:aug_sys}
\end{equation}
The gust-input column $\mathbf{b}_w$ appears in the top-right block of
$\tilde{\mA}$, so the DARE automatically infers the optimal feed-forward gain
on the estimated gust without any manual tuning.

A natural question is why the state is augmented with a gust model
$W_{k+1} = \lambda_w W_k$ that is known to be inaccurate: during the
gust ramp-up the true $W$ is rising, not decaying, so $\lambda_w = 0.98$
is a poor description of reality.
The purpose of the augmentation is \emph{not} to model the gust accurately.
It is to expose the causal channel $W \to \tilde{\vxi}$ to the DARE so
that the Riccati solver can compute the optimal feed-forward gain $K_w$.
Without augmentation, the term $\mathbf{b}_w W_k$ in \eqref{eq:lti} is an
exogenous disturbance invisible to the optimiser, and the resulting gain
$\mK$ reacts only to states that have already grown large.
By including $W_k$ as a state with dynamics $W_{k+1} = \lambda_w W_k$,
the DARE treats it as a quantity to be regulated and computes how much flap
deflection each unit of estimated gust warrants---this is $K_w$.
The specific value $\lambda_w = 0.98$ is a placeholder that encodes only
a slow-decay prior; what matters is that $\mathbf{b}_w$ appears in
$\tilde{\mA}$, making the gust--state coupling visible to the solver.
The online estimate $\hat{W}_k$ from the EKF is imperfect, especially near
the gust onset, but $-K_w \hat{W}_k$ still provides a feed-forward
deflection proportional to the current gust estimate---a significant
improvement over a purely reactive LQR without augmentation.

\paragraph{Cost function.}
The infinite-horizon cost over the augmented state is
\begin{equation}
  J = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
      \Bigl[\tilde{\vxi}_k^\top \tilde{\mQ}\,\tilde{\vxi}_k
           + \delta u_k^\top \mR_{\rm aug}\,\delta u_k\Bigr],
  \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{\mQ} \in \mathbb{R}^{6\times6}$ is block-diagonal:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{\mQ} = \begin{bmatrix} \mQ_{\rm aug} & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0}^\top & Q_w \end{bmatrix},
  \qquad
  \mQ_{\rm aug} = \mQ + \mC_{y,6}^\top \mQ_y \mC_{y,6},
  \quad
  \mR_{\rm aug} = \mR + \mD_y^\top \mQ_y \mD_y,
  \label{eq:augmented}
\end{equation}
with $\mC_{y,6} = [\mC_y\;\mathbf{0}] \in \mathbb{R}^{2\times6}$ the output
Jacobian padded with a zero column for the $W$ state.
Expanding the state cost makes the $C_L$ intent explicit:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{\vxi}_k^\top \mQ_{\rm aug}\, \tilde{\vxi}_k
  = \tilde{\vxi}_k^\top \mQ\, \tilde{\vxi}_k
  + \underbrace{(\mC_{y,6}\,\tilde{\vxi}_k)^\top \mQ_y\, (\mC_{y,6}\,\tilde{\vxi}_k)}_{%
      \approx\; Q_{y,L}\,\Delta C_L^2 + Q_{y,M}\,\Delta C_M^2},
  \label{eq:cost_expand}
\end{equation}
so $\mQ_y$ adds a direct cost on $\Delta C_L$ and $\Delta C_M$ deviations,
independently of whether they arise from structural motion or from the gust
acting on a near-stationary wing.

The weight values used in this work are
\begin{equation}
  \mQ = \operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{1}{0.00428^2},\,0,\,\frac{5}{0.00823^2},\,0,\,0\right),
  \quad
  \mR = \left[\frac{1}{0.5^2}\right],
  \label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
normalised by the expected peak amplitudes of $h$ and $\alpha$ under a
$60\,\text{m/s}$ gust.
Setting $\mQ_y = 0$ in the baseline results does \emph{not} mean that $C_L$ is
ignored: the state penalty $\mQ$ penalises $h$ and $\alpha$, and since
$C_L \approx C_{L,\rm tr} + \mC_y\delta\vxi$, reducing structural oscillations
indirectly attenuates the $C_L$ deviation.
The direct $\mQ_y$ penalty becomes useful when aerodynamic loads matter in their
own right (e.g.\ certification limits on $C_M$), at the price of larger flap
effort.
No gust penalty is applied ($Q_w = 0$): the DARE infers the feed-forward gain
$K_w$ entirely through $\mathbf{b}_w$ in $\tilde{\mA}$.

\paragraph{Discrete Algebraic Riccati Equation.}
The unique positive semi-definite solution $\mP$ of the $6\times6$ DARE
\begin{equation}
  \mP = \tilde{\mQ}
        + \tilde{\mA}^\top \mP \tilde{\mA}
        - \tilde{\mA}^\top \mP \tilde{\mB}
          \bigl(\mR_{\rm aug} + \tilde{\mB}^\top \mP \tilde{\mB}\bigr)^{-1}
          \tilde{\mB}^\top \mP \tilde{\mA}
  \label{eq:dare}
\end{equation}
is the \emph{value function} of the LQR problem: $J^* =
\tilde{\vxi}_0^\top \mP\, \tilde{\vxi}_0$ for any initial condition
$\tilde{\vxi}_0$.
A large eigenvalue of $\mP$ along a particular mode means that deviations in
that direction accumulate high future cost and should be corrected aggressively.

Because \eqref{eq:dare} is a fixed-point equation quadratic in $\mP$, naive
iteration diverges for lightly-damped systems.
The standard approach reformulates it as finding the stable invariant subspace
of the $12\times12$ symplectic pencil
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{H} =
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \tilde{\mA} & \tilde{\mB}\mR_{\rm aug}^{-1}\tilde{\mB}^\top \\
    -\tilde{\mQ} & -\tilde{\mA}^{-\top}
  \end{bmatrix},
  \label{eq:symplectic}
\end{equation}
whose $2n = 12$ eigenvalues come in reciprocal pairs $(\lambda_i, 1/\lambda_i)$,
so exactly $n = 6$ lie inside the unit disk.
The \texttt{scipy.linalg.solve\_discrete\_are} solver computes a Schur
decomposition $\mathbf{H} = \mathbf{Z}\mathbf{T}\mathbf{Z}^\top$, reorders it
so the stable eigenvectors appear first, and partitions the leading $n$ columns
as $[U_1^\top, U_2^\top]^\top$ to give
\begin{equation}
  \mP = U_2\, U_1^{-1}.
  \label{eq:P_schur}
\end{equation}
For the $6\times6$ system considered here the solve is instantaneous and
performed once at design time.

\paragraph{Optimal gain and control law.}
The gain is extracted from $\mP$ via
\begin{equation}
  \mK = \bigl(\mR_{\rm aug} + \tilde{\mB}^\top \mP\, \tilde{\mB}\bigr)^{-1}
        \tilde{\mB}^\top \mP\, \tilde{\mA},
  \label{eq:gain_formula}
\end{equation}
and partitions naturally into two blocks:
\begin{equation}
  \delta_k = -\mK_\xi\,\hat{\vxi}_k - K_w\,\hat{W}_k
  = -K_{\xi,1}\hat{h}
    - K_{\xi,2}\hat{\dot{h}}
    - K_{\xi,3}\hat{\alpha}
    - K_{\xi,4}\hat{\dot{\alpha}}
    - K_{\xi,5}\hat{z}
    - K_w\hat{W}_k.
  \label{eq:law_expanded}
\end{equation}
The feedback block $\mK_\xi \in \mathbb{R}^{1\times5}$ reacts to structural
deviations that have already developed.
The feed-forward scalar $K_w$ deflects the flap in response to the estimated
gust even when all structural states are at trim---providing the closest
approximation to anticipatory action available to a static linear gain.
Note that $K_w$ is distinct from $\lambda_w = 0.98$: $\lambda_w$ is a fixed
modelling assumption about gust decay, whereas $K_w$ is the optimal gain
computed from the cost matrices.

The control law requires all six components of
$\hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_k = [\hat{h},\hat{\dot{h}},\hat{\alpha},\hat{\dot{\alpha}},\hat{z},\hat{W}]^\top$.
The physical sensor outputs are
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{y}_k = \bigl[\ddot{h}_k,\;\alpha_k\bigr]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^2,
  \label{eq:meas_vec}
\end{equation}
where $\ddot{h}_k$ comes from an onboard accelerometer and $\alpha_k$ from an
angle-of-attack (AoA) sensor.
The lift coefficient $C_{L,k}$ is measured separately (pressure tap or force
balance) and enters the observer through the $C_L$\nobreakdash-inversion
branch.
The remaining states $\hat{z}$ and $\hat{W}$ are unobserved and reconstructed
by the EKF (Section~\ref{sec:ekf}).

\paragraph{Operation sequence at each timestep $k$.}
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{$C_L$ bisection.}
        Given the previous state estimate $\hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_{k-1}$,
        the previous control command $\delta_{k-1}$, and the measured lift
        $C_{L,k}^{\rm meas}$, find $\hat{W}_k$ such that
        \begin{equation}
          f_{\rm rec}\!\left(\hat{z}_{k-1},\,\hat{\vxi}_{k-1},\,\delta_{k-1};\,
          \hat{W}_k\right) = C_{L,k}^{\rm meas}.
          \label{eq:cl_inversion}
        \end{equation}
        Because $C_L$ is monotonically increasing in $W$ over
        $[0,\,80]\,\text{m/s}$, the solution is unique and found by bisection:
        \begin{equation}
          W^{\rm mid} = \frac{W^- + W^+}{2}, \quad
          \varepsilon = f_{\rm rec}(\hat{z}_{k-1}, \hat{\vxi}_{k-1}, \delta_{k-1};\,
                        W^{\rm mid}) - C_{L,k}^{\rm meas},
          \label{eq:bisection_step}
        \end{equation}
        setting $W^- \leftarrow W^{\rm mid}$ if $\varepsilon < 0$, else
        $W^+ \leftarrow W^{\rm mid}$, until $W^+ - W^- < 0.5\,\text{m/s}$.
        The bracket halves at each iteration, requiring at most
        $\lceil \log_2(80/0.5) \rceil = 8$ LDNet evaluations.
        The result $\hat{W}_k$ is injected into the EKF as a pseudo-measurement.

  \item \textbf{EKF predict.}
        The full nonlinear propagator $F_{\rm nl}$ advances the state estimate:
        \begin{equation}
          \hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_{k|k-1} = F_{\rm nl}(\hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_{k-1|k-1},\,\delta_{k-1}).
          \label{eq:ekf_pred_arch}
        \end{equation}
        The error covariance is propagated linearly using the Jacobian
        $\mathbf{F}_k$ recomputed at the current operating point.

  \item \textbf{EKF update.}
        The innovation $\mathbf{y}_k -
        \mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}\,\hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_{k|k-1}$ (augmented with the
        $\hat{W}_k$ pseudo-measurement) corrects the prediction via the Kalman
        gain $\mathbf{L}$, yielding $\hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_{k|k}$.
        Full details are given in Section~\ref{sec:ekf}.

  \item \textbf{LQR control law.}
        \begin{equation}
          \delta_k = -\mK_\xi\,\hat{\vxi}_k - K_w\,\hat{W}_k.
          \label{eq:law}
        \end{equation}

  \item \textbf{Plant step.}
        The true system advances under $\delta_k$ and the unknown true gust
        $W_k$, producing the next sensor readings $\mathbf{y}_{k+1}$ and
        $C_{L,k+1}^{\rm meas}$.
\end{enumerate}

% ================================================================
\subsubsection{Extended Kalman Filter Observer}
\label{sec:ekf}
% ================================================================

The EKF implements steps~2--3 of the online loop; we now detail its equations.
It propagates the full nonlinear model in the prediction step and applies an
optimal linear correction in the update step, providing principled noise
rejection and drift correction for the heave states.

\paragraph{State and measurements.}
The EKF operates on the augmented state
$\tilde{\vxi} = [h,\dot{h},\alpha,\dot{\alpha},z,W]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^6$.
Two signals feed into the observer at each timestep through distinct channels:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The measurement vector $\mathbf{y} = [\ddot{h},\,\alpha]^\top$
        enters the Kalman update step and corrects the state estimate via the
        innovation $\mathbf{y}_k - \mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}\,\hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_{k|k-1}$.
  \item The lift coefficient $C_L$ is used outside the filter, in the $C_L$
        bisection \eqref{eq:cl_inversion}--\eqref{eq:bisection_step}, to
        produce $\hat{W}_k$, which is then injected as a pseudo-measurement:
        \begin{equation}
          \mathbf{y}^{\rm aug}_k
          = \bigl[\ddot{h}_k,\;\alpha_k,\;\hat{W}_k\bigr]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^3,
          \qquad
          \mathbf{C}^{\rm aug} =
          \begin{bmatrix} \mathbf{C}_{\rm obs} \\ \mathbf{e}_6^\top \end{bmatrix}
          \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times6},
          \label{eq:aug_meas}
        \end{equation}
        where $\mathbf{e}_6^\top = [0,0,0,0,0,1]$ selects $W$ from
        $\tilde{\vxi}$.
        This lets the Kalman gain weight the bisection result against the
        filter's prediction with $R_W$ as the accuracy tuning parameter.
\end{itemize}

The output matrix $\mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}$ maps $\tilde{\vxi}$ to the measurement
space:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{C}_{\rm obs} =
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \mathbf{c}_{\ddot{h}}^\top \\
    \mathbf{e}_3^\top
  \end{bmatrix}
  \in \mathbb{R}^{2\times6},
  \label{eq:C_obs}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{e}_3^\top = [0,0,1,0,0,0]$ selects $\alpha$ directly.
The first row $\mathbf{c}_{\ddot{h}}^\top$ is the linearised sensitivity of
$\ddot{h}$ to $\tilde{\vxi}$ at trim, obtained by differentiating
\eqref{eq:eom}:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{c}_{\ddot{h}}^\top =
  \frac{1}{\det\mathbf{M}}\Bigl[
    -M_{\alpha\alpha}(K_h,\,D_h,\,-M_{h\alpha}K_\alpha,\,-M_{h\alpha}D_\alpha,\,0)
    - q_\infty(M_{\alpha\alpha}\,\mathbf{c}_{L} - M_{h\alpha}\,\mathbf{c}_{M})
  \Bigr]^\top,
  \label{eq:c_hddot}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{c}_L = \mC_y[0,:]$ and $\mathbf{c}_M = \mC_y[1,:]$ the rows of
the aerodynamic output Jacobian \eqref{eq:jacobians_out}.
This row is used only in the offline DARE solve; the online update uses the
full $\mathbf{C}^{\rm aug}$ \eqref{eq:aug_meas}.

\paragraph{Prediction.}
The full nonlinear propagator advances the state estimate:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_{k|k-1} = F_{\rm nl}(\hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_{k-1|k-1},\,\delta_{k-1}),
  \label{eq:ekf_pred}
\end{equation}
where $F_{\rm nl}$ is the plant propagator \eqref{eq:propagator} augmented with
the gust decay $W_{k+1} = \lambda_w W_k$.
Using the nonlinear predictor avoids linearisation error when $z$ deviates far
from trim during a strong gust.
The \emph{error covariance} $\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{k|k} \in \mathbb{R}^{6\times6}$ tracks
the uncertainty of the state estimate: its $(i,j)$ entry is the expected
cross-correlation of the $i$-th and $j$-th estimation errors.
It is entirely distinct from the value function $\mathbf{P}$ of the LQR DARE
(Section~\ref{sec:lqr}), which measures closed-loop cost-to-go.
The error covariance is propagated linearly:
\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{k|k-1} = \mathbf{F}_k\,\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{k-1|k-1}\,\mathbf{F}_k^\top + \mathbf{Q}_{\rm kf},
  \label{eq:ekf_cov_pred}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{F}_k = \partial F_{\rm nl}/\partial\tilde{\vxi}\big|_{\hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_{k-1|k-1}}$
is the Jacobian of the nonlinear propagator, recomputed at every step by automatic
differentiation (distinct from the fixed augmented system matrix $\tilde{\mA}$
used in the LQR DARE).

\paragraph{Update.}
The Kalman gain is
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{K}_{\rm EKF}
  = \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{k|k-1}\,{\mathbf{C}^{\rm aug}}^\top
    \bigl(\mathbf{C}^{\rm aug}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{k|k-1}{\mathbf{C}^{\rm aug}}^\top
          + \mathbf{R}^{\rm aug}\bigr)^{-1}
  \in \mathbb{R}^{6\times3},
  \label{eq:kalman_gain_ekf}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{R}^{\rm aug} = \operatorname{diag}(R_{\ddot{h}}, R_\alpha, R_W)$
collects the noise variances for the accelerometer, AoA sensor, and bisection
inversion.
The state is corrected as
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_{k|k}
  = \hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_{k|k-1}
  + \mathbf{K}_{\rm EKF}\,
    \bigl(\mathbf{y}^{\rm aug}_k
          - \mathbf{C}^{\rm aug}\,\hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_{k|k-1}\bigr),
  \label{eq:ekf_update}
\end{equation}
and the error covariance is updated in \emph{Joseph form} for numerical stability:
\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{k|k}
  = \bigl(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{K}_{\rm EKF}\mathbf{C}^{\rm aug}\bigr)\,
    \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_{k|k-1}\,
    \bigl(\mathbf{I} - \mathbf{K}_{\rm EKF}\mathbf{C}^{\rm aug}\bigr)^\top
  + \mathbf{K}_{\rm EKF}\,\mathbf{R}^{\rm aug}\,\mathbf{K}_{\rm EKF}^\top,
  \label{eq:joseph}
\end{equation}
which guarantees positive semi-definiteness under finite-precision arithmetic.

\paragraph{Observer DARE.}
The steady-state Kalman gain $\mathbf{L}$ is computed once offline by solving
the dual of the DARE \eqref{eq:dare}---the observer Riccati equation:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{S} = \mathbf{Q}_{\rm kf}
    + \tilde{\mA}\,\mathbf{S}\,\tilde{\mA}^\top
    - \tilde{\mA}\,\mathbf{S}\,\mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}^\top
      \bigl(\mathbf{R}_{\rm kf} + \mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}\,\mathbf{S}\,\mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}^\top\bigr)^{-1}
      \mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}\,\mathbf{S}\,\tilde{\mA}^\top,
  \label{eq:dare_obs}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{S} \in \mathbb{R}^{6\times6}$ is the steady-state error covariance
(the observer counterpart of the value function $\mathbf{P}$ in the controller DARE),
$\mathbf{Q}_{\rm kf} = \operatorname{diag}(10^{-6},10^{-6},10^{-6},10^{-6},10^{-8},0.1)$
is the process noise covariance, and
$\mathbf{R}_{\rm kf} = \operatorname{diag}((10^{-2})^2,(10^{-3})^2,(5\times10^{-3})^2)$
is the measurement noise covariance (accelerometer, AoA encoder, AoA rate).
The steady-state gain is then
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{L} = \mathbf{S}\,\mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}^\top
    \bigl(\mathbf{R}_{\rm kf} + \mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}\,\mathbf{S}\,\mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}^\top\bigr)^{-1}
  \in \mathbb{R}^{6\times3}.
  \label{eq:kalman_gain}
\end{equation}
The dual DARE \eqref{eq:dare_obs} is the transpose of \eqref{eq:dare} with
$(\tilde{\mA}, \tilde{\mB}, \tilde{\mQ})$ replaced by
$(\tilde{\mA}^\top, \mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}^\top, \mathbf{Q}_{\rm kf})$,
so the same \texttt{scipy.linalg.solve\_discrete\_are} solver applies.

\subsubsection{Simulation Results}

\paragraph{Setup.}
Open and closed configurations are compared under a single 1-cosine gust
\begin{equation}
  W(t) = \frac{W_0}{2}\Bigl(1 - \cos\tfrac{2\pi t}{T_g}\Bigr),
  \quad 0 \le t \le T_g,
\end{equation}
with $W_0 = 60\,\text{m/s}$, $T_g = 1\,\text{s}$, $U_\infty = 75\,\text{m/s}$:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Baseline} --- open-loop, no flap actuation.
  \item \textbf{LQR + EKF} --- Extended Kalman Filter observer
        (Section~\ref{sec:ekf}).
\end{enumerate}
