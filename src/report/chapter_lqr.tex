\subsection{Closed-Loop Results with LQR}
%Time histories of plunge, pitch and flap deflection under LQR control, comparison with the open-loop baseline, sensitivity to Q/R weights, discussion of trade-off between oscillation reduction and control effort.

Gust load alleviation (GLA) aims to reduce the structural response of an
aeroelastic system to atmospheric disturbances through active flap deflection.
Model Predictive Control (MPC) provides excellent performance by rolling out
an explicit nonlinear preview over a receding horizon, but requires solving an
online optimisation at every timestep.
Linear Quadratic Regulator (LQR) offers an attractive alternative: the optimal
feedback gain is computed offline once, at the expense of restricting
optimality to the neighbourhood of the trim point.

The plant considered here is the \emph{LDNet} model---a data-driven aeroelastic
emulator that couples a two-degree-of-freedom (2-DOF) structural wing with a
neural-network aerodynamic surrogate.
Because the aerodynamic model is a trained neural network, closed-form
Jacobians are unavailable; they are instead obtained through \emph{automatic
differentiation} (AD) of the full discrete-time propagator.
The resulting linearised system is then used to solve the Discrete Algebraic
Riccati Equation (DARE) and extract the optimal gain matrix $\mK$.

\subsubsection{LQR Architecture and Role of the Observer}
\label{sec:lqr_arch}
% ================================================================

Figure~\ref{fig:lqr_block} shows the closed-loop architecture.
At each timestep $k$ the controller receives the estimated augmented state
$\hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_k = [\hat{\vxi}_k^\top,\hat{W}_k]^\top$ from the
\emph{observer} and computes the flap command via the linear law
\eqref{eq:law}.
The plant (LDNet + structural dynamics) then evolves under that command and
produces the next measurable outputs ($\ddot{h}$, $\alpha$, $\dot{\alpha}$, $C_L$),
which feed back into the observer.

The gain law $\delta_k = -\mK\hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_k$ requires all six components
of $\hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_k = [\hat{h},\hat{\dot{h}},\hat{\alpha},\hat{\dot{\alpha}},\hat{z},\hat{W}]^\top$
at each timestep.
The measurement vector available to the observer is
$\mathbf{y} = [\ddot{h},\,\alpha,\,\dot{\alpha},\,C_L]^\top$: the heave
acceleration $\ddot{h}$ from an onboard accelerometer, the pitch angle $\alpha$
and pitch rate $\dot{\alpha}$ from an AoA sensor, and the lift coefficient $C_L$
from a pressure tap or force balance.
The states $\hat{z}$ and $\hat{W}$ are not measured and must be reconstructed,
as described below.


The pitch states $\alpha_k$ and $\dot{\alpha}_k$ are taken directly from the
AoA sensor.
The heave velocity and displacement are unobserved and reconstructed by a
\emph{leaky kinematic integrator} applied to the measured acceleration:
\begin{align}
  \hat{\dot{h}}_{k+1} &= \ell\,\hat{\dot{h}}_k + \ddot{h}_k\,\Delta t, \label{eq:leak_hd}\\
  \hat{h}_{k+1}       &= \ell\,\hat{h}_k       + \hat{\dot{h}}_{k+1}\,\Delta t, \label{eq:leak_h}
\end{align}
where $\ell = 1 - \Delta t/\tau$, $\tau = 5\,\text{s}$, is the leak factor.
The multiplicative leak prevents unbounded growth due to accelerometer bias or
numerical drift, at the cost of a slow low-frequency attenuation (corner
frequency $f_c = 1/(2\pi\tau) \approx 0.03\,\text{Hz}$, well below the
aeroelastic modes at $\sim\!3\,\text{Hz}$).


The gust velocity $\hat{W}_k$ is not measured directly but inferred by
\emph{inverting the aerodynamic model}: given the current state estimate
$\hat{\vxi}_k$, flap angle $\delta_k$, and measured lift $C_{L,k}^{\rm meas}$,
find $\hat{W}$ such that
\begin{equation}
  f_{\rm rec}\!\left(z_k, \hat{h}, \hat{\dot{h}}, \hat{\alpha},
                \hat{\dot{\alpha}}, \delta_k;\,\theta_r\right) = C_{L,k}^{\rm meas},
  \label{eq:cl_inversion}
\end{equation}
where $f_{\rm rec}$ is evaluated with $W = \hat{W}$ propagated through $f_{\rm dyn}$.
Because $C_L$ is monotonically increasing in $W$ for the operating range
$W \in [0,\,80]\,\text{m/s}$ (a property verified on the LDNet training data),
equation \eqref{eq:cl_inversion} has a unique solution, found by the following
\emph{bisection} procedure.
Initialise the bracket $[W^-, W^+] = [0, 80]\,\text{m/s}$ and iterate:
\begin{equation}
  W^{\rm mid} = \frac{W^- + W^+}{2}, \qquad
  \varepsilon = f_{\rm rec}(z_k, \hat{\vxi}_k, \delta_k;\,W^{\rm mid},\theta_r) - C_{L,k}^{\rm meas},
  \label{eq:bisection_step}
\end{equation}
setting $W^- \leftarrow W^{\rm mid}$ if $\varepsilon < 0$, else
$W^+ \leftarrow W^{\rm mid}$, until $W^+ - W^- < \varepsilon_{\rm tol} = 0.5\,\text{m/s}$.
The bracket halves at each iteration, so convergence requires at most
$\lceil \log_2(80/0.5) \rceil = 8$ LDNet evaluations per timestep---negligible
compared to the observer and control computations.

The gain $\mK$ is not chosen by trial and error but is the \emph{unique}
solution to an optimisation problem: minimise the infinite-horizon quadratic
cost $J$ in \eqref{eq:cost} subject to the linear dynamics \eqref{eq:aug_sys}.
The necessary condition for optimality is the DARE \eqref{eq:dare}, whose
solution $\mP$ is the \emph{value function} of the problem---the matrix such
that $J^* = \tilde{\vxi}_0^\top \mP\, \tilde{\vxi}_0$ for any initial condition
$\tilde{\vxi}_0$.
Once $\mP$ is known, the optimal gain follows algebraically from
\eqref{eq:gain}.
Intuitively, $\mP$ encodes how costly it is to be in each direction of state
space: a large eigenvalue of $\mP$ along a particular mode means that
deviations in that direction accumulate high future cost and should be corrected
aggressively.

\subsubsection{Extended Kalman Filter Observer}
\label{sec:ekf}
% ================================================================

The EKF propagates the full nonlinear model in the prediction step and applies
an optimal linear correction in the update step, providing principled noise
rejection and drift correction for the heave states.

\paragraph{State and measurements.}
The EKF operates on the augmented state
$\tilde{\vxi} = [h,\dot{h},\alpha,\dot{\alpha},z,W]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^6$.
Two signals feed into the observer at each timestep, through distinct channels:
\begin{itemize}
  \item The measurement vector $\mathbf{y} = [\ddot{h},\,\alpha,\,\dot{\alpha}]^\top$
        enters the Kalman update step directly and corrects the state estimate
        via the innovation $\mathbf{y}_k - \mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}\,\hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_{k|k-1}$.
  \item The lift coefficient $C_L$ is used \emph{outside} the filter, in the
        $C_L$ bisection procedure \eqref{eq:cl_inversion}--\eqref{eq:bisection_step},
        to produce the gust estimate $\hat{W}_k$.
        This estimate overwrites the $W$ component of the state directly,
        bypassing the Kalman correction for that component.
\end{itemize}

The output matrix $\mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}$ maps the state $\tilde{\vxi}$ into
the measurement space, allowing the filter to compute the \emph{predicted
measurement} $\mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}\,\hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_{k|k-1}$ --- that is,
what the sensors \emph{should} read if the current state estimate were correct.
The difference with the actual measurement $\mathbf{y}_k$ is the innovation,
which drives the state correction.
Its structure is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{C}_{\rm obs} =
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \mathbf{c}_{\ddot{h}}^\top \\
    \mathbf{e}_3^\top \\
    \mathbf{e}_4^\top
  \end{bmatrix}
  \in \mathbb{R}^{3\times6},
  \label{eq:C_obs}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{e}_3, \mathbf{e}_4$ are standard basis vectors that select
$\alpha$ and $\dot{\alpha}$ directly from $\tilde{\vxi}$ (since these states
are measured directly by the AoA sensor, their predicted measurement is trivially
the corresponding state component).

The first row $\mathbf{c}_{\ddot{h}}^\top$ is more subtle.
Although $\ddot{h}$ is measured directly by an accelerometer, it is
\emph{not} a state component---it is an output that depends on the full state
via the structural equations \eqref{eq:rhs}.
Linearising those equations at trim gives the explicit dependence of $\ddot{h}$
on $\tilde{\vxi}$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{c}_{\ddot{h}}^\top =
  \frac{1}{\det\mathbf{M}}\Bigl[
    -M_{\alpha\alpha}(K_h,\,D_h,\,-M_{h\alpha}K_\alpha,\,-M_{h\alpha}D_\alpha,\,0)
    - q_\infty(M_{\alpha\alpha}\,\mathbf{c}_{L} - M_{h\alpha}\,\mathbf{c}_{M})
  \Bigr]^\top,
  \label{eq:c_hddot}
\end{equation}
with $\mathbf{c}_L = \mC_y[0,:]$ and $\mathbf{c}_M = \mC_y[1,:]$ the first and
second rows of the aerodynamic output Jacobian \eqref{eq:jacobians_out}.
This row is needed so that the DARE solver can compute the optimal Kalman gain
$\mathbf{L}$ accounting for the full measurement set including $\ddot{h}$.
However, because \eqref{eq:c_hddot} relies on $\mC_y$ evaluated at trim, it
becomes inaccurate when $z$ deviates significantly from trim during a strong
gust.
For this reason $\mathbf{c}_{\ddot{h}}$ is used \emph{only} in the offline
DARE solve; the online update step uses only the pitch rows
$[\mathbf{e}_3^\top;\,\mathbf{e}_4^\top]$, as detailed in the next paragraph.



At each timestep the predicted state is obtained by running the full nonlinear
propagator---LDNet plus the structural RK4 integrator---on the current estimate:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_{k|k-1} = F_{\rm nl}(\hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_{k-1|k-1},\,\delta_{k-1}),
  \label{eq:ekf_pred}
\end{equation}
where $F_{\rm nl}$ is identical to the plant propagator \eqref{eq:propagator}
augmented with the gust decay $W_{k+1} = \lambda_w W_k$.
Using the nonlinear predictor avoids the linearisation error that would arise
from applying $\tilde{\mA}$ when $z$ deviates far from trim during a strong gust.


The correction uses only the pitch measurements (rows 1--2 of
$\mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}$), because the heave-acceleration row \eqref{eq:c_hddot}
depends on $\mC_y$ evaluated at trim and becomes inaccurate for large $z$
excursions:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_{k|k}
  = \hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_{k|k-1}
  + \mathbf{L}_{1:2}\,
    \bigl(\mathbf{y}_{1:2,k} - \mathbf{C}_{\rm obs,1:2}\,\hat{\tilde{\vxi}}_{k|k-1}\bigr),
  \label{eq:ekf_update}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{y}_{1:2} = [\alpha,\dot{\alpha}]^\top$ and
$\mathbf{L}_{1:2} \in \mathbb{R}^{6\times2}$ are the corresponding columns of
the Kalman gain matrix $\mathbf{L}$.
The $W$ row of $\mathbf{L}$ is zeroed: the gust estimate is always set by
$C_L$ bisection \eqref{eq:cl_inversion}, which is more reliable than the
filter's $W$ correction when $C_y$ is evaluated far from trim. Details of the computation of the Kalman gain will be given in Section ???

\subsubsection{Structural Dynamics}

The wing is modelled as a rigid section with two degrees of freedom: heave
displacement $h$ (positive downward) and pitch angle $\alpha$ (positive
nose-up).  Aerodynamic forces are transmitted to the structure via a trailing-edge
flap with deflection angle $\delta$.

The equations of motion in coupled form are
\begin{equation}
  \begin{bmatrix} M_{hh} & M_{h\alpha} \\ M_{h\alpha} & M_{\alpha\alpha} \end{bmatrix}
  \begin{bmatrix} \ddot{h} \\ \ddot{\alpha} \end{bmatrix}
  =
  \begin{bmatrix} -F_y - D_h\dot{h} - K_h h \\ M_z - D_\alpha\dot{\alpha} - K_\alpha\alpha \end{bmatrix},
  \label{eq:eom}
\end{equation}
where $F_y = q_\infty C_L$ and $M_z = q_\infty C_M$ are the aerodynamic lift
force and pitching moment, $q_\infty = \tfrac{1}{2}\rho U_\infty^2 S$ is the
dynamic pressure ($S = 0.05\,\text{m}^2$, $\rho = 1.225\,\text{kg/m}^3$), and
the inertial coupling arises from the off-centre flap mass.

\subsubsection{Neural Aerodynamic Surrogate (LDNet)}

The aerodynamic coefficients $C_L$ and $C_M$ are produced by a recurrent
neural network with a scalar latent state $z \in \mathbb{R}$:
\begin{align}
  z_{k+1} &= z_k + \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta t_{\rm ref}}\,f_{\rm dyn}(z_k, h_k, \dot{h}_k, \alpha_k, \dot{\alpha}_k, \delta_k, W_k;\,\theta_d),
  \label{eq:zdyn}\\
  \begin{bmatrix} C_L \\ C_M \end{bmatrix}_k &= f_{\rm rec}(z_k, h_k, \dot{h}_k, \alpha_k, \dot{\alpha}_k, \delta_k;\,\theta_r),
  \label{eq:yrec}
\end{align}
where $W_k$ is the gust velocity, $f_{\rm dyn}$ is a shallow MLP (7-7-1), and
$f_{\rm rec}$ is a deeper network (24-24-24-24-2).
Both networks are kept frozen during control design.

The full state is the concatenation of structural and latent variables:
\begin{equation}
  \vxi_k = \begin{bmatrix} h_k & \dot{h}_k & \alpha_k & \dot{\alpha}_k & z_k \end{bmatrix}^\top \in \mathbb{R}^5.
\end{equation}
The trim condition is defined at free-stream velocity $U_\infty = 75\,\text{m/s}$
with zero gust ($W = 0$) and zero structural displacement.
The latent state $z_{\rm tr}$ is obtained by iterating \eqref{eq:zdyn} to
steady state with $\delta = 0$; at trim $C_{L,\rm tr} \approx 0.031$ and
$C_{M,\rm tr} \approx 0$.
The trim point is therefore
\begin{equation}
  \vxi_{\rm tr} = \begin{bmatrix} 0 & 0 & 0 & 0 & z_{\rm tr} \end{bmatrix}^\top, \quad
  u_{\rm tr} = \delta_{\rm tr} = 0.
\end{equation}

\section{Jacobian Linearisation via Automatic Differentiation}
\label{sec:jacobian}
% ================================================================

\subsubsection{Discrete-Time Propagator}

The combined structural integrator and aerodynamic network define a
discrete-time propagator
\begin{equation}
  \vxi_{k+1} = F(\vxi_k, u_k),
  \label{eq:propagator}
\end{equation}
where $F: \mathbb{R}^5 \times \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}^5$ is a composite map
evaluated in two sequential stages:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Aerodynamic step (LDNet).}  Given $(\vxi_k, u_k)$, the two
        sub-networks of LDNet are evaluated in sequence.
        First, $f_{\rm dyn}$ advances the latent aerodynamic state:
        \begin{equation*}
          z_{k+1} = z_k + \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta t_{\rm ref}}\,
                    f_{\rm dyn}(z_k, h_k, \dot{h}_k, \alpha_k, \dot{\alpha}_k,
                                u_k, W{=}0;\,\theta_d),
        \end{equation*}
        where $\theta_d$ denotes the frozen weights of $f_{\rm dyn}$, and
        $W = 0$ because the linearisation is performed at trim (no gust).
        Then $f_{\rm rec}$ reads out the aerodynamic coefficients from the
        \emph{current} latent state $z_k$ (not yet updated):
        \begin{equation*}
          \begin{bmatrix} C_L \\ C_M \end{bmatrix}_k
          = f_{\rm rec}(z_k, h_k, \dot{h}_k, \alpha_k, \dot{\alpha}_k,
                        u_k;\,\theta_r),
        \end{equation*}
        where $\theta_r$ denotes the frozen weights of $f_{\rm rec}$.

  \item \textbf{Structural step (RK4).}  The aerodynamic forces
        $F_y = q_\infty C_L$ and $M_z = q_\infty C_M$ computed in step~1 are
        held fixed and used to drive the structural equations \eqref{eq:eom}
        over one timestep $\Delta t = 0.01\,\text{s}$ via a fourth-order
        Runge--Kutta scheme.
        Defining the structural state $\bm{x} = [h,\dot{h},\alpha,\dot{\alpha}]^\top$,
        the right-hand side is
        \begin{align}
          \dot{\bm{x}} &= \bm{g}(\bm{x}, F_y, M_z), \nonumber\\
          g_1 &= \dot{h},\quad
          g_2 = \frac{M_{\alpha\alpha}(-F_y - D_h\dot{h} - K_h h)
                      - M_{h\alpha}(M_z - D_\alpha\dot{\alpha} - K_\alpha\alpha)}
                     {M_{hh}M_{\alpha\alpha} - M_{h\alpha}^2},\nonumber\\
          g_3 &= \dot{\alpha},\quad
          g_4 = \frac{M_{hh}(M_z - D_\alpha\dot{\alpha} - K_\alpha\alpha)
                      - M_{h\alpha}(-F_y - D_h\dot{h} - K_h h)}
                     {M_{hh}M_{\alpha\alpha} - M_{h\alpha}^2},
          \label{eq:rhs}
        \end{align}
        where $\det \mathbf{M} = M_{hh}M_{\alpha\alpha} - M_{h\alpha}^2$.
        RK4 integrates $\bm{g}$ over $\Delta t$ to produce
        $[h_{k+1}, \dot{h}_{k+1}, \alpha_{k+1}, \dot{\alpha}_{k+1}]^\top$.
\end{enumerate}

Combining both steps, the full next state is
\begin{equation}
  \vxi_{k+1} = \bigl[\,
    \underbrace{h_{k+1},\,\dot{h}_{k+1},\,\alpha_{k+1},\,\dot{\alpha}_{k+1}}_{\text{step 2: RK4}},\;
    \underbrace{z_{k+1}}_{\text{step 1: }f_{\rm dyn}}
  \,\bigr]^\top.
  \label{eq:state_assembly}
\end{equation}
The aerodynamic coefficients $(C_L, C_M)_k$ are a byproduct of step~1:
they are computed inside $f_{\rm rec}$ before the structural integration and
require no additional network evaluation.
They serve a dual role: as the forcing of the structural integrator in step~2,
and as the aerodynamic output of the propagator at timestep $k$:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{c}_k = \begin{bmatrix} C_L \\ C_M \end{bmatrix}_k
  = f_{\rm rec}(\vxi_k, u_k;\,\theta_r).
  \label{eq:aero_output}
\end{equation}




\subsubsection{Five Jacobian Matrices}

Linearising \eqref{eq:propagator} and the output map around the trim point
$(\vxi_{\rm tr}, 0)$, and including the sensitivity to the gust velocity $W$,
yields the discrete-time LTI system
\begin{equation}
  \delta\vxi_{k+1} = \mA_d\,\delta\vxi_k + \mB_d\,\delta u_k + \mathbf{b}_w\,W_k, \qquad
  \delta\vy_k = \mC_y\,\delta\vxi_k + \mD_y\,\delta u_k,
  \label{eq:lti}
\end{equation}
where $\delta\vxi = \vxi - \vxi_{\rm tr}$, $\delta u = u - u_{\rm tr}$, and
\begin{equation}
  \mA_d = \frac{\partial F}{\partial \vxi}\bigg|_{\rm tr} \in \mathbb{R}^{5\times5},\quad
  \mB_d = \frac{\partial F}{\partial u}\bigg|_{\rm tr} \in \mathbb{R}^{5\times1},\quad
  \mathbf{b}_w = \frac{\partial F}{\partial W}\bigg|_{\rm tr} \in \mathbb{R}^{5},
  \label{eq:jacobians}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}

  \mC_y = \frac{\partial g}{\partial \vxi}\bigg|_{\rm tr} \in \mathbb{R}^{2\times5},\quad
  \mD_y = \frac{\partial g}{\partial u}\bigg|_{\rm tr} \in \mathbb{R}^{2\times1}.
  \label{eq:jacobians_out}
\end{equation}
The gust-input vector $\mathbf{b}_w$ is obtained by declaring $W$ as an additional
\texttt{tf.Variable} inside the same tape and calling \texttt{tape.jacobian}
on $\vxi_{k+1}$ with respect to it, at no extra computational cost.

\paragraph{Trim computation and latent-state initialisation.}
The structural components of $\vxi_{\rm tr}$ are set to zero by definition.
The latent state $z_{\rm tr}$ requires more care: in principle it should satisfy
the fixed-point condition $f_{\rm dyn}(z_{\rm tr}, \mathbf{0}, 0, 0;\,\theta_d) = 0$,
i.e.\ $\dot{z} = 0$ under zero structural motion and zero gust.
In practice this fixed point need not exist for a network trained exclusively on
dynamic trajectories, and numerical experiments confirm that iterating
\eqref{eq:zdyn} with all inputs set to zero does \emph{not} converge to a
stationary value: $\|z_{k+1} - z_k\|$ fails to decrease below $\mathcal{O}(10^{-3})$
even after thousands of steps, and root-finding via Levenberg--Marquardt yields
a residual $\|f_{\rm dyn}(z^*, \mathbf{0})\| \approx 0.8$, confirming that no
exact fixed point exists in the reachable region.

In implementation, $z_{\rm tr}$ is therefore obtained by running 200 warm-up steps
of \eqref{eq:zdyn} from $z_0 = 0$ with all inputs zero, which gives a
practically stationary transient value $z_{\rm tr} \approx -0.46$.
The resulting aerodynamic coefficients are $C_{L,\rm tr} \approx 0.035$ and
$C_{M,\rm tr} \approx 0.031$.
Two consequences follow from this approximate trim:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Jacobian accuracy.} $C_{L,\rm tr}$ and $C_{M,\rm tr}$ enter the
        linearisation only as additive constants that are subtracted before
        differentiation; they therefore cancel in the Jacobian and do not affect
        $\mA_d$, $\mB_d$, $\mC_y$, $\mD_y$, or $\mathbf{b}_w$.
  \item \textbf{Control bias.} $z_{\rm tr}$ enters the run-time law \eqref{eq:law}
        as a reference subtracted from the observer estimate $\hat{z}_k$.
        An error $\Delta z = z_{\rm tr,approx} - z_{\rm tr,true}$ produces a
        constant deflection bias $\Delta\delta = -K_z\,\Delta z$, where $K_z$
        is the fifth component of $\mK_\xi$.
        Numerically, $K_z \approx -1.36$ and $|z_{\rm tr}| \approx 0.46$, giving
        $|\Delta\delta| \lesssim 0.6^\circ$ out of the $\pm20^\circ$ saturation
        limit---approximately $3\%$ of the actuator range.
        Moreover, the EKF propagates $z$ in absolute coordinates and converges
        rapidly from the warm-up initial guess, so $\hat{z}_k - z_{\rm tr}$ at
        operating conditions is dominated by the true gust-induced excursion
        rather than the trim offset, and the bias has negligible effect on
        closed-loop performance.
\end{enumerate}



\subsubsection{Computation via Reverse-Mode Automatic Differentiation}

Because $F$ passes through the LDNet weights, closed-form expressions for the
partial derivatives in \eqref{eq:jacobians} are not available.
All five matrices are instead obtained analytically---without finite
differences---by \emph{reverse-mode automatic differentiation} (AD) applied to
the full discrete-time map
\begin{equation}
  \Phi : (\vxi,\, u,\, W) \;\longmapsto\;
  \bigl[\,F(\vxi, u, W)^\top,\;\; g(\vxi, u)^\top\,\bigr]^\top
  \in \mathbb{R}^{7},
  \label{eq:Phi}
\end{equation}
evaluated at the trim point $(\vxi_{\rm tr}, 0, 0)$.

Here $F(\vxi, u, W) \in \mathbb{R}^5$ collects the five state components at
$k+1$---namely $[h_{k+1}, \dot{h}_{k+1}, \alpha_{k+1}, \dot{\alpha}_{k+1},
z_{k+1}]^\top$---produced by the two-step propagator \eqref{eq:propagator}.
The map $g(\vxi, u) = [C_L, C_M]_k^\top \in \mathbb{R}^2$ collects the
aerodynamic coefficients that are computed as a byproduct of step~1 and used
to force the structural integrator in step~2.
Both are computed in the same forward pass; stacking them into $\Phi \in
\mathbb{R}^7$ allows all five Jacobian matrices $\mA_d, \mB_d, \mathbf{b}_w,
\mC_y, \mD_y$ to be obtained in a single automatic differentiation call.



Both the RK4 integrator \eqref{eq:rhs} and the two neural-network layers
$f_{\rm dyn}$, $f_{\rm rec}$ are finite compositions of differentiable
primitive operations (affine maps, element-wise activations, additions).
The full map $\Phi$ can therefore be written as
\begin{equation}
  \Phi(\vxi, u, W)
  = \phi_K \circ \phi_{K-1} \circ \cdots \circ \phi_1(\vxi, u, W),
  \label{eq:chain}
\end{equation}
where each $\phi_k : \mathbb{R}^{n_k} \to \mathbb{R}^{n_{k+1}}$ is one of two
types of primitive operation whose Jacobian is trivial by construction:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Affine layer.} $\phi_k(\mathbf{x}) = \mathbf{W}_k\mathbf{x} + \mathbf{b}_k$
        is linear, so $\mathbf{J}_k = \mathbf{W}_k$ exactly---the weight matrix
        already stored in memory, evaluated at zero cost.
  \item \textbf{Element-wise activation.} $\phi_k(\mathbf{x}) = \sigma(\mathbf{x})$
        applied component-by-component gives a diagonal Jacobian
        $\mathbf{J}_k = \operatorname{diag}(\sigma'(x_1),\ldots,\sigma'(x_n))$,
        where $\sigma'$ is the scalar derivative of the activation function
        (e.g.\ $\sigma'(x) = 1 - \tanh^2(x)$ for a $\tanh$ unit,
        already computed during the forward pass).
\end{itemize}
No global differentiation of $F$ or $g$ is required: the chain rule assembles
these local primitives automatically.


To compute the $i$-th row of the Jacobian $\partial\Phi/\partial\vxi$,
reverse-mode AD performs a single \emph{forward pass} to record the
intermediate values $\phi_1, \ldots, \phi_K$, then a single \emph{backward
pass} that accumulates the adjoint vector
$\bar{\mathbf{v}} \in \mathbb{R}^{n_k}$ from output to input via the
transpose chain rule:
\begin{equation}
  \bar{\mathbf{v}}_{k-1}
  = \mathbf{J}_k^\top\,\bar{\mathbf{v}}_k,
  \qquad
  \bar{\mathbf{v}}_K = \mathbf{e}_i,
  \label{eq:adjoint}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{e}_i$ is the $i$-th standard basis vector.
After $K$ steps, $\bar{\mathbf{v}}_0 = \partial\Phi_i/\partial\vxi$.
Repeating for each of the 7 output components of $\Phi$ yields all five
Jacobian matrices in \eqref{eq:jacobians}--\eqref{eq:jacobians_out} exactly,
at a cost of 7 backward passes through the same computational graph---far
cheaper than $5+1+1 = 7$ forward finite-difference perturbations and with
no truncation error.

These two types cover all operations in both LDNet sub-networks
($f_{\rm dyn}$ and $f_{\rm rec}$).
After the aerodynamic step, $\Psi_j$ applies the structural RK4 integrator,
which computes the four slope estimates
\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
        k_1 &= g(x_j,\, F_y,\, M_z), \quad & k_2 &= g\!\left(x_j + \tfrac{\Delta t}{2}k_1,\, F_y,\, M_z\right), \\
        k_3 &= g\!\left(x_j + \tfrac{\Delta t}{2}k_2,\, F_y,\, M_z\right), \quad & k_4 &= g(x_j + \Delta t\,k_3,\, F_y,\, M_z),
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:rk4_slopes}
\end{equation}
and then updates the structural state as
$x_{j+1} = x_j + \tfrac{\Delta t}{6}(k_1 + 2k_2 + 2k_3 + k_4)$.
The right-hand side $g$ in \eqref{eq:rhs} is \emph{linear} in the structural
state $x = [h, \dot{h}, \alpha, \dot{\alpha}]^\top$: it consists of linear
combinations of the state components plus the aerodynamic forces $F_y$ and
$M_z$, which arrive from LDNet and are held constant during the RK4 sub-steps.
Each slope $k_i$ is therefore an affine function of $x_j$---exactly the same
type of primitive as an affine layer in the network.
From the perspective of automatic differentiation, RK4 requires no special
treatment: TensorFlow records each sub-step as an additional affine operation
in the computation graph and differentiates through all four stages
automatically, in exactly the same way it differentiates through the network
layers.
Composing $N$ such steps gives the rollout map

The open-loop eigenvalues $\lambda_i(\mA_d)$ all satisfy $|\lambda_i| < 1$,
confirming that the trim equilibrium is asymptotically stable in the
linearised sense, albeit with low modal damping
($|\lambda_i| \approx 0.97$--$0.99$ for the aeroelastic modes).

\subsubsection{Gust-Augmented State and Cost Function}

To exploit the estimated gust velocity $\hat{W}_k$ as a feed-forward signal,
the state vector is extended with a gust state.  A first-order exponential
decay is assumed as the gust model:
\begin{equation}
  W_{k+1} = \lambda_w\, W_k, \qquad \lambda_w = 0.98,
  \label{eq:gust_model}
\end{equation}
giving the augmented state $\tilde{\vxi}_k = [\vxi_k^\top,\, W_k]^\top \in \mathbb{R}^6$
and the augmented system
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{\vxi}_{k+1}
  =
  \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} \mA_d & \mathbf{b}_w \\ \mathbf{0}^\top & \lambda_w \end{bmatrix}}_{\tilde{\mA} \in \mathbb{R}^{6\times6}}
  \tilde{\vxi}_k
  +
  \underbrace{\begin{bmatrix} \mB_d \\ 0 \end{bmatrix}}_{\tilde{\mB} \in \mathbb{R}^{6\times1}}
  \delta_k.
  \label{eq:aug_sys}
\end{equation}
The gust-input column $\mathbf{b}_w$ (computed in \eqref{eq:jacobians}) appears
in the top-right block of $\tilde{\mA}$, so the DARE automatically infers the
optimal feed-forward gain on the estimated gust.

The infinite-horizon cost over the augmented state is
\begin{equation}
  J = \sum_{k=0}^{\infty}
      \Bigl[\tilde{\vxi}_k^\top \tilde{\mQ}\,\tilde{\vxi}_k
           + \delta u_k^\top \mR_{\rm aug}\,\delta u_k\Bigr],
  \label{eq:cost}
\end{equation}
where $\tilde{\mQ} \in \mathbb{R}^{6\times6}$ is block-diagonal:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{\mQ} = \begin{bmatrix} \mQ_{\rm aug} & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0}^\top & Q_w \end{bmatrix},
  \qquad
  \mQ_{\rm aug} = \mQ + \mC_{y,6}^\top \mQ_y \mC_{y,6},
  \quad
  \mR_{\rm aug} = \mR + \mD_y^\top \mQ_y \mD_y,
  \label{eq:augmented}
\end{equation}
with $\mC_{y,6} = [\mC_y\;\mathbf{0}] \in \mathbb{R}^{2\times6}$ (the output
Jacobian padded with a zero column for the W state, since $W$ does not appear
directly in $f_{\rm rec}$), and $Q_w \ge 0$ an optional penalty on the gust state.

The role of $\mQ_y$ becomes transparent by expanding the state cost:
\begin{equation}
  \tilde{\vxi}_k^\top \mQ_{\rm aug}\, \tilde{\vxi}_k
  = \tilde{\vxi}_k^\top \mQ\, \tilde{\vxi}_k
  + \underbrace{(\mC_{y,6}\,\tilde{\vxi}_k)^\top \mQ_y\, (\mC_{y,6}\,\tilde{\vxi}_k)}_{%
      \approx\,\delta\vy_k^\top \mQ_y\,\delta\vy_k
      \,=\, Q_{y,L}\,\Delta C_L^2 + Q_{y,M}\,\Delta C_M^2},
  \label{eq:cost_expand}
\end{equation}
where $\delta\vy_k = \mC_{y,6}\,\tilde{\vxi}_k \approx [C_{L,k} - C_{L,\rm tr},\; C_{M,k} - C_{M,\rm tr}]^\top$
is the linearised aerodynamic deviation from trim.
Penalising $\tilde{\vxi}$ with $\mC_{y,6}^\top \mQ_y \mC_{y,6}$ is therefore
equivalent (to first order) to penalising $C_L$ and $C_M$ deviations directly,
without augmenting the state vector.

The weight values used in this work are
\begin{equation}
  \mQ = \operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{1}{0.00428^2},\,0,\,\frac{5}{0.00823^2},\,0,\,0\right),
  \quad
  \mR = \left[\frac{1}{0.5^2}\right],
  \label{eq:weights}
\end{equation}
normalised by the expected peak amplitudes of $h$ and $\alpha$ under a
$60\,\text{m/s}$ gust.

Setting $\mQ_y = 0$ does \emph{not} mean that $C_L$ and $C_M$ are ignored.
The state penalty $\mQ$ already penalises $h$ and $\alpha$, and since
$C_L \approx C_{L,\rm tr} + \mC_y\delta\vxi$ (from the output Jacobian),
reducing structural oscillations indirectly attenuates aerodynamic excursions.
The output penalty $\mQ_y$ adds a \emph{direct} cost on $\Delta C_L$ and
$\Delta C_M$, independently of whether they are caused by structural motion or
by the gust acting on a near-stationary wing.
It becomes useful when the aerodynamic loads matter in their own right
(e.g., fatigue or certification limits on $C_M$), at the price of a larger
flap effort; this trade-off is discussed in Section~\ref{sec:sensitivity}.
No gust penalty ($Q_w = 0$) is applied either, meaning the DARE optimises the
feed-forward gain $K_w$ purely through the coupling introduced by $\mathbf{b}_w$
in $\tilde{\mA}$.

\subsubsection{Discrete Algebraic Riccati Equation}

The solution to the infinite-horizon LQR problem for the augmented system
\eqref{eq:aug_sys} is the unique positive semi-definite solution $\mP$ of the
$6\times6$ \emph{Discrete Algebraic Riccati Equation} (DARE):
\begin{equation}
  \mP = \tilde{\mQ}
        + \tilde{\mA}^\top \mP \tilde{\mA}
        - \tilde{\mA}^\top \mP \tilde{\mB}
          \bigl(\mR_{\rm aug} + \tilde{\mB}^\top \mP \tilde{\mB}\bigr)^{-1}
          \tilde{\mB}^\top \mP \tilde{\mA},
  \label{eq:dare}
\end{equation}
Because \eqref{eq:dare} is a fixed-point equation quadratic in $\mP$, it cannot
be solved by direct inversion or simple iteration---naive fixed-point iteration
$\mP \leftarrow \tilde{\mQ} + \ldots$ diverges for unstable or lightly-damped
systems.
The standard approach reformulates the problem as a linear eigenvalue problem
on a larger matrix, the \emph{symplectic pencil}.


The key observation is that the DARE \eqref{eq:dare} is equivalent to finding
the stable invariant subspace of the $2n \times 2n$ matrix
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{H} =
  \begin{bmatrix}
    \tilde{\mA} & \tilde{\mB}\mR_{\rm aug}^{-1}\tilde{\mB}^\top \\
    -\tilde{\mQ} & -\tilde{\mA}^{-\top}
  \end{bmatrix}
  \in \mathbb{R}^{12\times12},
  \label{eq:symplectic}
\end{equation}
called the \emph{symplectic pencil} (or Hamiltonian matrix in the discrete-time
case).
Intuitively, $\mathbf{H}$ couples the forward dynamics $\tilde{\mA}$ with the
backward dynamics $-\tilde{\mA}^{-\top}$: its $2n = 12$ eigenvalues come in
reciprocal pairs $(\lambda_i,\, 1/\lambda_i)$, so exactly $n = 6$ of them lie
inside the unit disk and $n = 6$ lie outside.
The $n$ eigenvalues inside the unit disk correspond to the \emph{stable}
directions of the closed-loop system, and the associated eigenvectors encode
the solution $\mP$.


The \emph{Schur decomposition} of $\mathbf{H}$ factors it as
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{H} = \mathbf{Z}\,\mathbf{T}\,\mathbf{Z}^\top,
  \label{eq:schur}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{Z} \in \mathbb{R}^{12\times12}$ is orthogonal
($\mathbf{Z}^\top\mathbf{Z} = \mathbf{I}$) and $\mathbf{T}$ is quasi-upper
triangular with the eigenvalues of $\mathbf{H}$ on its diagonal.
The Schur decomposition is numerically stable and can be computed in
$\mathcal{O}(n^3)$ floating-point operations via the QR algorithm, with no
iteration on $\mP$ itself.

\texttt{scipy.linalg.solve\_discrete\_are} reorders the Schur form so that the
$n = 6$ columns of $\mathbf{Z}$ associated with eigenvalues \emph{inside} the
unit disk appear first.
Partitioning these $n$ columns as $[U_1^\top,\, U_2^\top]^\top$ with
$U_1, U_2 \in \mathbb{R}^{6\times6}$ gives the unique stabilising solution:
\begin{equation}
  \mP = U_2\, U_1^{-1}.
  \label{eq:P_schur}
\end{equation}
Intuitively, $U_1$ and $U_2$ span the stable subspace of $\mathbf{H}$: $U_1$
describes how the state evolves forward in time, and $U_2$ describes the
associated cost-to-go, so their ratio $U_2 U_1^{-1}$ is exactly the value
function matrix $\mP$ that maps any initial state $\tilde{\vxi}_0$ to the
optimal cost $J^* = \tilde{\vxi}_0^\top \mP\, \tilde{\vxi}_0$.
For the $6\times6$ system considered here the solve is instantaneous and
performed once at design time.


\subsection{Optimal Gain and Control Law}

The gain $\mK$ partitions naturally into two blocks with distinct physical
roles.
The first block $\mK_\xi \in \mathbb{R}^{1\times5}$ contains one scalar gain
per state component of $\hat{\vxi}_t = [\hat{h}, \hat{\dot{h}}, \hat{\alpha},
\hat{\dot{\alpha}}, \hat{z}]^\top$: each entry specifies how many degrees of
flap deflection are commanded per unit deviation of the corresponding state
from trim.

The gain is extracted from the DARE solution $\mP$ via
\begin{equation}
  \mK = \bigl(\mR_{\rm aug} + \tilde{\mB}^\top \mP\, \tilde{\mB}\bigr)^{-1}
        \tilde{\mB}^\top \mP\, \tilde{\mA},
  \label{eq:gain_formula}
\end{equation}
where the term $\mR_{\rm aug} + \tilde{\mB}^\top \mP\, \tilde{\mB}$ is the
\emph{effective control cost}: it combines the explicit penalty $\mR_{\rm aug}$
on deflection with the additional cost $\tilde{\mB}^\top \mP\, \tilde{\mB}$
incurred by propagating a unit control input through the value function $\mP$.
Its inverse weights the cross-term $\tilde{\mB}^\top \mP\, \tilde{\mA}$, which
measures how much each state component, when propagated one step forward by
$\tilde{\mA}$, projects onto the control-input direction $\tilde{\mB}$ in the
cost metric defined by $\mP$.
In the scalar-input case considered here all terms reduce to scalars or
row/column vectors, so \eqref{eq:gain_formula} requires only one scalar
division.

Expanding the control law explicitly:
\begin{equation}
  \delta_t = -K_{\xi,1}\hat{h}
             - K_{\xi,2}\hat{\dot{h}}
             - K_{\xi,3}\hat{\alpha}
             - K_{\xi,4}\hat{\dot{\alpha}}
             - K_{\xi,5}\hat{z}
             - K_w\hat{W}_t.
  \label{eq:law_expanded}
\end{equation}

The second block $K_w \in \mathbb{R}$ is a single scalar that acts as a
\emph{feed-forward} gain on the estimated gust velocity $\hat{W}_t$.
Unlike the first five terms, which react to structural deviations that have
already developed, the $K_w\hat{W}_t$ term deflects the flap in response to
the estimated gust even when all structural states are at trim---providing the
closest approximation to anticipatory action that a static linear gain can
achieve.
Note that $K_w$ is distinct from the gust decay parameter $\lambda_w = 0.98$
in \eqref{eq:gust_model}: $\lambda_w$ is a fixed modelling assumption about
how fast the gust decays, whereas $K_w$ is the optimal feed-forward gain
computed by the DARE from the cost matrices $\tilde{\mQ}$ and $\mR_{\rm aug}$.


The gain $\mathbf{L}$ is computed once offline by solving the \emph{dual} of
the DARE \eqref{eq:dare}---the observer Riccati equation:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{S} = \mathbf{Q}_{\rm kf}
    + \tilde{\mA}\,\mathbf{S}\,\tilde{\mA}^\top
    - \tilde{\mA}\,\mathbf{S}\,\mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}^\top
      \bigl(\mathbf{R}_{\rm kf} + \mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}\,\mathbf{S}\,\mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}^\top\bigr)^{-1}
      \mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}\,\mathbf{S}\,\tilde{\mA}^\top,
  \label{eq:dare_obs}
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{Q}_{\rm kf} = \operatorname{diag}(10^{-6},10^{-6},10^{-6},10^{-6},10^{-8},0.1)$
is the process noise covariance and
$\mathbf{R}_{\rm kf} = \operatorname{diag}((10^{-2})^2,(10^{-3})^2,(5\times10^{-3})^2)$
is the measurement noise covariance (accelerometer, AoA encoder, AoA rate).
The steady-state gain is then
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{L} = \mathbf{S}\,\mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}^\top
    \bigl(\mathbf{R}_{\rm kf} + \mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}\,\mathbf{S}\,\mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}^\top\bigr)^{-1}
  \in \mathbb{R}^{6\times3}.
  \label{eq:kalman_gain}
\end{equation}
The dual DARE \eqref{eq:dare_obs} is the transpose of \eqref{eq:dare} with
$(\tilde{\mA}, \tilde{\mB}, \tilde{\mQ})$ replaced by
$(\tilde{\mA}^\top, \mathbf{C}_{\rm obs}^\top, \mathbf{Q}_{\rm kf})$,
so the same \texttt{scipy.linalg.solve\_discrete\_are} solver and Schur
decomposition apply.
